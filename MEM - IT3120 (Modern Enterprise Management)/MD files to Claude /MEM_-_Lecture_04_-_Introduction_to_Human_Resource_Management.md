## Page 28 — Key Components of Performance Management

### Original Slide Content
Key Components of Performance Management

- Performance Planning

Setting clear goals, expectations, and performance standards aligned with  organizational objectives.

- Performance Measurement

Assessing employee performance using key performance indicators (KPIs) and  agreed criteria.

- Feedback and Communication

Providing continuous feedback and maintaining open communication between  managers and employees.

- Performance Review (Appraisal)

Periodically evaluating performance to identify achievements and areas for  improvement.

- Employee Development

Identifying skill gaps and supporting training and development for future  performance.

# Full Lecture Summary

Human Resource Management (HRM) is presented as a **strategic** approach to managing people as organizational assets. Its purpose is not limited to administration: HRM aligns employee skills, behaviour, development, and performance with business objectives. In the IT industry this is especially important because organizational success depends heavily on skilled knowledge workers, rapid learning, cross-functional project teams, and the ability to retain talent in a competitive labour market.

The lecture identifies major HRM elements and functions including **employment/recruitment, development, performance management, reward management, employee well-being, employee relations, and resourcing strategy**. Recruitment begins with workforce planning and job analysis, continues through sourcing, screening, testing/interviews, and final selection, and ends with job offer, acceptance, onboarding, and induction. Modern recruitment increasingly uses digital platforms, e-recruitment systems, analytics, employer branding, remote/global hiring, and candidate-experience practices.

Performance management is a **continuous cycle** of planning, acting, monitoring, reviewing, and development. It uses goals, standards, KPIs, communication, feedback, appraisal, employee development, and reward/recognition to improve individual and organizational results. Reward management uses financial, non-financial, intrinsic, and extrinsic rewards. A **total reward** approach combines pay and benefits with recognition, career development, flexibility, and work-life balance.

Leadership is distinguished from management: leadership emphasizes **inspiration, influence, people, vision, and change**, while management emphasizes **planning, organizing, control, tasks, resources, and stability**. Leadership styles covered are autocratic, democratic, and transformational. Agile team leadership facilitates self-management and removes blockers, whereas traditional management more directly assigns and controls work.

Negotiation in HR is used to reach acceptable agreements on workplace matters. The lecture distinguishes collaborative **win-win**, competitive **win-lose**, and compromising negotiation. Organizational power is the ability to influence others and may arise from formal position, expertise, or personal trust and relationships.

Project teams are temporary, goal-focused groups of people with complementary skills. Effective teams require clear goals, defined roles, communication, trust, leadership, commitment, and accountability. Common challenges include communication problems, role ambiguity, conflict, low trust, poor leadership, and low commitment.

Motivation influences employee direction, effort, and persistence. Personality influences workplace behaviour, fit, communication, teamwork, leadership potential, and performance. The Big Five traits covered are openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and emotional stability. The IFS Sri Lanka case study connects these HRM concepts to IT-sector problems such as turnover, global competition, burnout, and continuous reskilling.

<a id="likely-exam-questions-and-short-answers"></a>
# Likely Exam Questions and Short Answers

### Question 1 — Define Human Resource Management.
**Expected answer:** HRM is a strategic and coherent approach to managing people as key organizational assets. It includes recruiting, hiring, deploying, developing, and managing employees so their efforts support organizational goals.

### Question 2 — Why is HRM especially important in the IT industry?
**Expected answer:** IT depends on skilled knowledge workers and rapid technology change. HRM helps attract and retain talent, align skills with strategy, support learning, manage performance, motivate employees, strengthen teams, and manage change.

### Question 3 — List the recruitment process in order.
**Expected answer:** Workforce planning → job analysis → sourcing candidates → screening applications → selection methods → final selection decision → job offer and acceptance → onboarding and induction.

### Question 4 — Explain the performance management cycle.
**Expected answer:** Planning sets goals and standards; acting is task execution; monitoring tracks progress; reviewing evaluates results and feedback; development addresses training needs; the process then repeats.

### Question 5 — Differentiate financial, non-financial, intrinsic, and extrinsic rewards.
**Expected answer:** Financial rewards are monetary; non-financial rewards are non-cash organizational benefits; intrinsic rewards are internal satisfaction from the work itself; extrinsic rewards are externally provided rewards such as promotion or recognition.

### Question 6 — Differentiate leadership and management.
**Expected answer:** Leadership focuses on inspiring and influencing people toward a shared vision and change. Management focuses on planning, organizing, controlling resources, and maintaining stable operations.

### Question 7 — Compare the three negotiation types.
**Expected answer:** Collaborative negotiation seeks win-win outcomes; competitive negotiation seeks advantage for one side; compromising negotiation uses concessions to reach a middle-ground agreement.

### Question 8 — Name and explain three sources of power.
**Expected answer:** Legitimate power comes from formal position; expert power comes from knowledge and skills; referent/personal power comes from trust, respect, relationships, and personal traits.

### Question 9 — What makes a project team effective?
**Expected answer:** Clear goals, defined roles, effective communication, trust and cooperation, leadership/support, commitment, and accountability.

### Question 10 — What are the Big Five personality traits in this lecture?
**Expected answer:** Openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and emotional stability (neuroticism).

### Question 11 — Apply HRM solutions to the IFS Sri Lanka case.
**Expected answer:** Improve retention through competitive pay, career paths, bonuses, and flexible work; strengthen employer branding; improve well-being and recognition; provide continuous cloud/AI training; and integrate performance, communication, and HR strategy.
