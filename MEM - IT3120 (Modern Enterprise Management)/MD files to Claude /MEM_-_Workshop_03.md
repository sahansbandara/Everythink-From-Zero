## Page 01 - Workshop Objective and Presentation Requirements

### Original Page Content

**IT3120 - Modern Enterprise Management**  
**Entrepreneurship for ICT Professionals**  
**Workshop 3 - Entrepreneurship for ICT Professionals**

### Objective

This activity enables students to apply entrepreneurship concepts learned during the lecture by analyzing real-world entrepreneurial scenarios within the Sri Lankan ICT context. Students will evaluate:

- Opportunities
- Challenges
- Risks
- Entrepreneurial processes
- Business growth strategies

### Presentation Requirements

**Choose Your Own Format!**

Groups are encouraged and welcome to present their findings and analysis using any creative, innovative format of their choice, as long as it covers all required content areas below within the **10-minute time**.

Examples of alternative formats include, but are not limited to:

#### Investor Pitch (Shark Tank style)

A set of group members plays the role of founders of the startup, and the rest of the members play a panel of investors.

- Spend about **5 minutes** pitching:
  - The problem
  - The opportunity
  - Why the business will succeed
- Then take **5 minutes** of tough follow-up questions from the investor panel about:
  - Funding
  - Risks
  - Growth plans

#### Podcast or Panel Discussion

Group members take on different roles. For example:

- Founder
- Skeptical investor
- Industry commentator

They hold a natural, conversational discussion about the case study's:

- Opportunity
- Challenges
- Strategy

The presentation should feel like an episode of a business podcast.

#### Interactive Prototype / Low-Fidelity Demo

The group builds a simple, clickable mockup of the app using a free tool such as:

- Figma
- Canva

The group then demonstrates the product live, explaining:

- How it solves the problem
- What business decisions shaped its design

#### Mock Board Meeting / Crisis Simulation

The group acts out an emergency board meeting called to respond to the core problem in the case.

Each member plays a role such as:

- CEO
- Operations lead
- Financial advisor

The group must discuss the situation and reach a decision live, in front of the class, rather than presenting pre-written conclusions.

### Explanation

The workshop is not asking for a standard slide presentation only. The format can be creative, but creativity does **not** replace the required entrepreneurship analysis. The chosen format must still communicate the problem, opportunity, risks, strategy, and growth recommendations clearly.

### Supplementary Definition

**Definition:** Entrepreneurial opportunity  
A business possibility where a real problem or unmet need can be addressed through a product, service, or business model.

**Simple meaning:** A problem exists, and a startup sees a useful way to solve it and create value.

### Exam Tip

For any case-study presentation, begin with this sequence:

```text
Problem -> Opportunity -> Risks -> Strategy -> Recommendations
```

If these five areas are clear, the presentation is easier to structure.

### Common Mistake

Choosing a creative format but failing to answer the required business questions will lose marks. The format is only the delivery method.

### Likely Exam / Workshop Questions

1. **Why does the workshop encourage alternative presentation formats?**  
   **Expected answer:** To encourage creativity, communication, audience engagement, and practical application of entrepreneurship concepts.

2. **What must remain unchanged regardless of presentation format?**  
   **Expected answer:** The group must still cover all required content areas and assessment criteria within the 10-minute time limit.

## Page 02 - Assessment Areas and Presentation Coverage

### Original Page Content

The continuation of the Mock Board Meeting / Crisis Simulation explains that each member may play a role such as CEO, operations lead, or financial advisor, and the group must discuss the situation and reach a decision live rather than presenting pre-written conclusions.

### Your Own Idea

If the group has another creative way to present the case, such as:

- A game
- A role-play
- A comic strip
- A live demo of another kind

the group is welcome to propose it.

Whichever format is chosen, all groups will be assessed against the same Evaluation Criteria, so the chosen format must clearly cover every required section.

Each group must prepare a **10-minute presentation** or alternative format covering the following areas:

### 1. Problem Identification (10%)

- What problem does the startup/business address?
- Why is this problem important?

### 2. Opportunity Analysis (15%)

- What entrepreneurial opportunity was identified?
- Which ICT opportunity area does it belong to?

### 3. Challenges and Risks (15%)

Discuss:

- Funding Challenges
- Market Risks
- Technical Risks
- Operational Risks
- Regulatory Challenges

### 4. Entrepreneurial Strategy (10%)

- How could Lean Startup principles be applied?
- How can risks be mitigated?
- How can the business achieve sustainable growth?

### 5. Recommendations (10%)

Provide **two recommendations** for future business growth.

### Helpful Analysis Table

| Required area | Main question to answer | Evidence to look for in a case |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Identification | What problem exists? | Customer pain, inefficiency, unmet need |
| Opportunity Analysis | What business opportunity exists? | ICT solution, target customer, value |
| Challenges and Risks | What could stop success? | Funding, market, technology, operations, regulation |
| Entrepreneurial Strategy | How should the startup respond? | Testing, learning, mitigation, growth plan |
| Recommendations | What should happen next? | Two realistic actions linked to the problem |

### Explanation

The assessment expects analysis, not only description. A weak answer says what the startup does. A stronger answer explains why the business opportunity exists, what can go wrong, and what strategy should be used to improve success.

### Supplementary Definitions

**Definition:** Market risk  
The possibility that customers may not adopt, continue using, or pay for the product.

**Simple meaning:** The startup may build something that people do not use enough.

**Definition:** Technical risk  
The possibility that the product's technology may fail, be difficult to use, be insecure, or be hard to integrate.

**Simple meaning:** The technical solution may not work reliably for real customers.

**Definition:** Operational risk  
Risk caused by failures in day-to-day business processes such as logistics, service delivery, staffing, or support.

**Simple meaning:** The business idea may be good, but daily operations may fail.

**Definition:** Regulatory risk  
Risk created by laws, compliance requirements, approval processes, or government procedures.

**Simple meaning:** The startup may face legal or official restrictions that delay or limit business.

### Exam Tip

Do not list risks randomly. Classify each risk under a clear category and explain its effect on the business.

### Common Mistake

A problem is not the same as a risk.

- **Problem:** The customer pain the startup is trying to solve.
- **Risk:** Something that may prevent the startup from succeeding.

### Likely Exam / Workshop Questions

1. **What five categories of risk does the workshop require students to discuss?**  
   **Expected answer:** Funding, market, technical, operational, and regulatory risks.

2. **What should the recommendation section contain?**  
   **Expected answer:** Two recommendations for future business growth.

### Original Page Content

### 6. Delivery Quality (10%)

The group will be assessed on:

- Clarity
- Creativity
- Team Participation
- Time Management

### 7. Innovative Approach (15%)

These marks reward how creatively and effectively the group communicated its findings, whichever format it chose.

The lecturer will consider:

- **Originality:** Did the group choose or design a format that goes beyond a standard slide presentation?
- **Fit for purpose:** Did the chosen format actually help communicate the case more clearly, rather than being creative for its own sake?
- **Execution:** Was the format prepared and delivered smoothly, such as good production quality, confident role-play, or a working prototype?
- **Audience engagement:** Did the format keep the class interested and involved, for example through questions, interaction, or visuals?

### Case Study 6 - AgroAI

A startup develops an AI-powered crop disease prediction system.

Although the technology works well, farmers find the system difficult to understand and use.

#### Discussion Questions

1. What technology challenge is evident?
2. What entrepreneurial assumptions may have been incorrect?
3. How can the startup apply the Build-Measure-Learn approach?
4. What improvements would you recommend?

### Visual Explanation

```text
BUILD
AI crop disease prediction system
        |
        v
MEASURE
Technology works, but farmers struggle to use it
        |
        v
LEARN
Technical accuracy alone is not enough
        |
        v
REBUILD / IMPROVE
Simpler interface + clearer outputs + farmer testing
```

### Supplementary Definition

**Definition:** Build-Measure-Learn  
A Lean Startup feedback cycle in which a startup builds a version of a solution, measures how real users respond, learns from the evidence, and improves the next version.

**Simple meaning:** Build something small, test it with users, learn what is wrong, and improve it.

### Supplementary Case Analysis

| Area | Answer direction |
|---|---|
| Technology challenge | Poor usability / difficulty understanding the system |
| Incorrect assumption | Assuming that a technically accurate solution would automatically be easy for farmers to adopt |
| Build | Create a simpler version/interface |
| Measure | Observe farmer use and collect feedback |
| Learn | Identify confusing features and language/interface problems |
| Improve | Simplify UX, explanations, local-language guidance, onboarding |

### Exam Tip

AgroAI is a good example of the difference between **technical performance** and **user usability**.

### Common Mistake

Do not say the technology is failing. The case explicitly says the technology works well; usability is the problem.

Workshop 3 focuses on applying entrepreneurship concepts to realistic ICT startup scenarios in Sri Lanka.

The workshop requires students to do more than describe a startup. Each case should be analyzed through a structured entrepreneurial lens:

Groups may use creative presentation formats such as an investor pitch, podcast/panel, low-fidelity prototype, board-meeting simulation, or their own format. Regardless of format, all required analytical sections must be covered within the 10-minute period.

The 16 cases demonstrate recurring entrepreneurship themes:

1. **Smart Farmer** - customer adoption and digital literacy.
2. **UniLearn** - user retention in online learning.
3. **HealthConnect** - trust in telemedicine.
4. **RideNow** - competition and differentiation.
5. **SecurePay** - privacy, cybersecurity, and FinTech trust.
6. **AgroAI** - usability, incorrect assumptions, and Build-Measure-Learn.
7. **TravelMate Sri Lanka** - tourism demand fluctuations and diversification.
8. **CyberShield** - low customer awareness and perceived value.
9. **CampusJobs** - monetization and business model challenges.
10. **LankaMarket** - logistics, operational risk, and customer complaints.

# Likely Exam Questions and Short Answers

### Question 1
What is the difference between a business problem and an entrepreneurial opportunity?

**Expected Answer:**  
A problem is an unmet customer need or pain point. An entrepreneurial opportunity is a viable way to solve that problem and create value through a product, service, or business model.

### Question 2
Name five risk areas required by the workshop.

**Expected Answer:**  
Funding, market, technical, operational, and regulatory risks.

### Question 3
Why is customer adoption important even when technology works?

**Expected Answer:**  
A technically successful product cannot become a sustainable business if target users are unwilling or unable to use it. AgroAI and Smart Farmer both demonstrate this issue.

### Question 4
Explain Build-Measure-Learn using AgroAI or AquaSense.

**Expected Answer:**  
Build a testable version, measure how real users respond, learn from usability/adoption evidence, improve the product or business model, and repeat.

### Question 5
Differentiate customer acquisition and retention.

**Expected Answer:**  
Acquisition means gaining new users or customers. Retention means keeping them active over time. UniLearn demonstrates a retention problem.

### Question 6
How can trust affect ICT entrepreneurship?

**Expected Answer:**  
If customers do not trust a platform's safety, reliability, privacy, or quality, they may refuse to adopt it. HealthConnect, SecurePay, and MediTrack demonstrate trust-related barriers.

### Question 7
What is the major risk in LankaMarket?

**Expected Answer:**  
Operational risk, especially delivery delays and logistics problems that increase customer complaints.

### Question 8
What business-model problem is visible in CampusJobs?

**Expected Answer:**  
The platform gains users but generates little revenue, showing weak monetization.

### Question 9
What is the main barrier to EduVR adoption?

**Expected Answer:**  
High VR headset cost and limited university budgets.

### Question 10
Why does GreenBin face slow commercialization?

**Expected Answer:**  
Municipal budget constraints and lengthy government procurement procedures delay implementation.

### Question 11
What is SkillBridge's key commercial challenge?

**Expected Answer:**  
Only a small percentage of registered users convert to paid premium subscriptions.

### Question 12
What is EventLink's main market risk?

**Expected Answer:**  
Seasonal demand causes usage and revenue to fluctuate outside peak event seasons.

### Question 13
How can a startup mitigate strong competitive pressure, as in RideNow?

**Expected Answer:**  
Differentiate on customer value, service quality, local focus, or unique features rather than relying only on discounts.

### Question 14
Why are privacy and cybersecurity especially important for SecurePay?

**Expected Answer:**  
Customers use the platform for financial transactions, so concerns about cyberattacks and data privacy directly affect trust and adoption.

### Question 15
What must a recommendation section contain according to the workshop?

**Expected Answer:**  
Two recommendations for future business growth.

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Describing the startup without identifying the underlying customer problem.
- Treating a feature as an entrepreneurial opportunity without explaining the value it creates.
- Listing risks without classifying or explaining them.
- Confusing market risk with technical risk.
- Confusing operational risk with technical risk.
- Saying high registrations automatically mean a successful business.
- Ignoring customer retention after acquisition.
- Assuming positive feedback automatically means customers will pay.
- Recommending price cuts without considering financial sustainability.
- Recommending international expansion before validating the business locally.
- Treating cybersecurity only as an IT issue instead of a trust/business issue.
- Ignoring regulatory and procurement delays in healthcare/government cases.
- Saying a product has failed technically when the case states that the technology works.
- Focusing on presentation creativity while missing required analytical sections.
- Ignoring the discrepancy between Page 02 percentage labels and the Page 03 evaluation table.
