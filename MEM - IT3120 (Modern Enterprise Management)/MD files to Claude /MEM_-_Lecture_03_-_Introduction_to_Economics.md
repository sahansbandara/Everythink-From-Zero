## Page 06 — What is Economics? Choices Under Scarcity

### Original Page Content

Economics studies how **people and organizations make choices when resources are limited but needs and wants are unlimited**.

Since resources are limited, societies must decide:

- What goods and services to produce.
- How to produce them.
- Who should receive them.

### Visual Explanation

The slide includes a small balance / economic-decision icon beside the three questions. The main emphasis is the bold statement about limited resources and unlimited needs and wants.

### Explanation

Scarcity forces three basic economic decisions:

```text
WHAT to produce?
        |
HOW to produce?
        |
WHO receives the output?
```

These decisions appear in households, businesses, and governments.

### Exam Tip

Memorize the three questions exactly:

1. What to produce?
2. How to produce?
3. Who should receive it?

### Common Mistake

Do not say scarcity means "nothing is available." Scarcity means resources are limited relative to needs and wants.

## Page 07 — Scarcity & the Economic Problem

### Original Page Content

### Finite Resources

Land, labor, and capital are limited in supply.

They:

- Cannot be produced on demand.
- Cannot be expanded infinitely.

### Infinite Wants

Human desires have no natural ceiling.

The gap between:

- What we have, and
- What we want

is the core economic problem.

### Opportunity Cost

Every choice carries a cost: **the value of the next-best alternative foregone when a decision is made.**

### Visual Explanation

The slide visually arranges:

```text
Finite Resources          Infinite Wants
       \                       /
        \                     /
         \                   /
          v                 v
          Scarcity / Economic Problem
                    |
                    v
             Opportunity Cost
```

A money / decision icon reinforces the opportunity-cost idea.

### Definition

**Definition:** Scarcity is the economic condition in which finite resources are insufficient to satisfy unlimited wants.

**Simple meaning:** There is not enough of everything to satisfy every possible desire.

### Definition

**Definition:** Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative foregone when a decision is made.

**Simple meaning:** What you give up by choosing one option instead of the best alternative.

### Exam Tip

The phrase **next-best alternative foregone** is the key wording.

### Common Mistake

Opportunity cost is not necessarily only money. It can be time, benefits, output, or another opportunity that was sacrificed.

## Page 09 — Neoclassical Market

### Original Page Content

A **neoclassical market** is a theoretical economic framework where prices, production, and consumption are driven by supply and demand.

Key ideas:

### Supply & Demand

The value of a good or service is derived from perceived usefulness to the consumer rather than just its cost.

### Rational Behavior

Buyers and sellers act logically and consistently, making choices that optimize their outcomes.

### Perfect Competition

Assumes:

- Numerous buyers and sellers.
- Identical products.
- No barriers to entry or exit.
- No single entity can dictate prices.

### Market Equilibrium

If there is a surplus or shortage, the market automatically adjusts prices until supply matches demand, resulting in the most efficient allocation of scarce resources.

### Visual Explanation

The slide is a text-based conceptual summary with four bold subtopics under the neoclassical-market definition.

### Definition

**Definition:** A neoclassical market is a theoretical framework in which supply and demand drive prices, production, and consumption.

**Simple meaning:** Prices and quantities are determined mainly by the interaction between buyers and sellers.

### Exam Tip

The four keywords are:

- Supply & Demand.
- Rational Behavior.
- Perfect Competition.
- Market Equilibrium.

### Common Mistake

Do not treat perfect competition as merely "many companies." The slide also assumes identical products and no entry or exit barriers.

## Page 12 — The Law of Demand

### Original Page Content

### The Inverse Relationship

As price rises, quantity demanded falls, and vice versa.

Consumers respond rationally to changing prices.

### What Shifts Demand?

- Changes in consumer income.
- Shifts in tastes and preferences.
- Prices of related goods:
  - Substitutes.
  - Complements.
- Future price expectations.

### Visual Explanation

The slide contains the law of demand on the left and a list of demand-shift factors below it. Decorative economic icons appear on the right.

### Definition

**Definition:** The law of demand states that, other things being equal, price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions.

**Simple meaning:** Higher price -> lower quantity demanded; lower price -> higher quantity demanded.

### Explanation

It is important to distinguish:

```text
Change in own price
      -> movement along demand curve

Change in non-price determinant
      -> shift of demand curve
```

### Exam Tip

A common MCQ asks which factor **shifts** demand. The source lists income, preferences, related-goods prices, and future price expectations.

### Common Mistake

Do not call every change in quantity demanded a "shift." A change caused only by the product's own price is a movement along the existing curve.

## Page 14 — Shifts in the Demand Curve

### Original Page Content

### Movement Along the Curve

Caused by a **change in price only**.

The curve itself stays fixed; the consumer simply moves to a different point on the same line.

### Rightward Shift ↑

Demand increases at every price level.

Causes:

- Rising incomes.
- Positive advertising.
- Population growth.

### Leftward Shift ↓

Demand decreases at every price level.

Causes:

- Recession.
- Cheaper substitutes.
- Changing consumer tastes.

### Visual Explanation

The slide is a three-part comparison:

| Situation | Meaning | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Movement along curve | Move to another point on same curve | Product's own price changes |
| Rightward shift | Demand rises at every price | Income, advertising, population |
| Leftward shift | Demand falls at every price | Recession, substitutes, tastes |

### Definition

**Definition:** A demand-curve shift is a change in demand at every price caused by a non-price determinant.

**Simple meaning:** The entire demand relationship changes.

### Exam Tip

Memory rule:

```text
Own price changes -> movement
Other determinants -> shift
```

### Common Mistake

Do not say a price change shifts the demand curve.

## Page 18 — Market Equilibrium

### Original Page Content

**Equilibrium is the price at which quantity supplied equals quantity demanded — the market "clears."**

- A surplus pushes prices down.
- A shortage pushes prices up.

### Visual Explanation

The graph plots:

- **Price** on the horizontal axis from `$2` to `$10`.
- Quantity values on the vertical scale from `0` to `100`.
- An upward line for **Quantity Supplied**.
- A downward line for **Quantity Demanded**.

The two lines intersect at approximately:

- Price: `$6`
- Quantity: `60`

The graph's visible values can be recreated as:

| Price | Quantity Supplied | Quantity Demanded |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $2 | 20 | 100 |
| $3 | 30 | 90 |
| $4 | 40 | 80 |
| $5 | 50 | 70 |
| $6 | 60 | 60 |
| $7 | 70 | 50 |
| $8 | 80 | 40 |
| $9 | 90 | 30 |
| $10 | 100 | 20 |

### Definition

**Definition:** Market equilibrium occurs where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded.

**Simple meaning:** Buyers want exactly the amount sellers want to sell at that price.

### Explanation

The adjustment logic is:

```text
Surplus -> downward pressure on price
Shortage -> upward pressure on price
Equilibrium -> Qs = Qd
```

### Exam Tip

Equilibrium requires equality of **quantities**, not simply a "fair" price.

### Common Mistake

Do not say equilibrium means demand and supply disappear. It means the quantities supplied and demanded are equal at the equilibrium price.

## Page 19 — Surplus and Shortage

### Original Page Content

At a price of **Rs 140**, a surplus occurs because the quantity of cups of coffee offered for sale is larger than the quantity people want to buy.

At a price of **Rs 80**, many people want to buy cups of coffee but few want to sell, so a shortage occurs.

Only at market equilibrium **point E** does quantity supplied equal quantity demanded.

### Visual Explanation

The graph has:

- Vertical axis: **Price of Coffee (Rs per Cup)**.
- Horizontal axis: **Cups of Coffee per Week**.
- Upward-sloping supply curve.
- Downward-sloping demand curve.
- Dashed price lines at:
  - Rs 140.
  - Rs 80.
- Equilibrium point `E`.

At **Rs 140**:

- Demand shown: `D = 600`.
- Supply shown: `S = 1000`.
- Surplus quantity:

```text
1000 - 600 = 400 cups
```

At **Rs 80**:

- Supply shown: `S = 400`.
- Demand shown: `D = 800`.
- Shortage quantity:

```text
800 - 400 = 400 cups
```

The exact numerical coordinates of point `E` are not printed on the graph, so they are not invented here.

### Definition

**Definition:** A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.

**Simple meaning:** Sellers offer more than buyers want.

### Definition

**Definition:** A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.

**Simple meaning:** Buyers want more than sellers offer.

### Exam Tip

To calculate:

```text
Surplus = Qs - Qd
Shortage = Qd - Qs
```

when the relevant quantities are known.

### Common Mistake

Do not reverse the surplus and shortage formulas.

## Page 20 — Elasticity: How Sensitive Are Buyers?

### Original Page Content

Elasticity measures how strongly buyers respond to price changes.

It determines:

- How firms price products.
- How taxes ripple through markets.

### Elastic

Demand drops sharply with price hikes.

The visual example says:

- **Luxury handbags**.
- Consumers are very responsive to price changes and switch to alternatives.

### Inelastic

Demand stays stable regardless of price.

The visual example says:

- **Insulin and gasoline**.
- Consumers buy roughly the same amount regardless of price increases.

### Visual Explanation

The slide is split into two halves:

```text
ELASTIC                           INELASTIC
Price changes                     Price changes
     |                                 |
     v                                 v
Large change in quantity          Small change in quantity
```

The graphic uses arrows and price-tag icons to emphasize sensitivity versus stability.

### Definition

**Definition:** Elasticity measures how strongly buyers respond to price changes.

**Simple meaning:** It tells us how sensitive quantity demanded is to price.

### Definition

**Definition:** Elastic demand changes strongly when price changes.

**Simple meaning:** Buyers react a lot to price.

### Definition

**Definition:** Inelastic demand changes relatively little when price changes.

**Simple meaning:** Buyers continue buying roughly similar amounts despite price changes.

### Exam Tip

Examples shown on the slide:

- Elastic: luxury handbags.

# Full Lecture Summary

Economics studies how society manages scarce resources. Resources such as land, labor, capital, technology, and time are limited, while human needs and wants are extensive. Because of scarcity, society must decide what to produce, how to produce it, and who should receive the output.

A key consequence of scarcity is **opportunity cost**: the value of the next-best alternative forgone when a decision is made. This principle applies directly to business and software decisions, such as choosing between developing a new mobile application and improving an existing product.

Markets bring buyers and sellers together physically or virtually. In the neoclassical model, prices, production, and consumption are driven by supply and demand. Households and firms interact through factor markets and product markets.

The **law of demand** describes an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. A change in the product's own price creates movement along a demand curve, whereas non-price determinants such as income, tastes, related-goods prices, population, advertising, and expectations can shift demand.

The **law of supply** states that higher prices encourage producers to supply more. Supply can shift because of input costs, technology, the number of sellers, and government policy.

**Market equilibrium** occurs where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. If price is above the equilibrium level, the market can experience a surplus. If price is below equilibrium, a shortage can occur. Surpluses create downward pressure on price, while shortages create upward pressure.

**Elasticity** measures how strongly buyers respond to price changes. Elastic demand changes sharply with price, whereas inelastic demand stays relatively stable.

Production costs are divided into **fixed costs** and **variable costs**. Fixed costs do not vary with output in the short run, while variable costs change with production. **Marginal cost** is the extra total cost associated with additional output.

Average-cost measures include:

- AFC: fixed cost per unit.
- AVC: variable cost per unit.
- ATC: total cost per unit.

The lecture illustrates diminishing marginal product using a cookie-production example. As additional workers are hired, the marginal product of labor falls. The corresponding total-cost curve becomes steeper as output grows.

The coffee-shop example shows:

- AFC falls continuously.
- AVC rises.
- MC rises.
- ATC is U-shaped.
- MC crosses ATC at the minimum of ATC.

The lecture also explains how AI can support cost analysis by processing large datasets, monitoring cost changes, predicting variable and marginal costs, identifying inefficiencies, and supporting decisions.

Economic benefits are discussed through **consumer surplus** and **producer surplus**. Consumer surplus is the difference between willingness to pay and actual price. Producer surplus is the difference between price received and production cost. Their sum represents total economic benefit in the market.

| Marginal Cost | Change in total cost from additional output | Extra cost of more output |
| Production Cost | Direct and indirect expense of producing a good/service | Total expense of output |
| AFC | Fixed cost divided by output | Fixed cost per unit |
| AVC | Variable cost divided by output | Variable cost per unit |
| ATC | Total cost divided by output | Total cost per unit |
| Marginal Product of Labor | Additional output from an additional worker | Extra output from next worker |
| Diminishing Marginal Product | Marginal product falls as more input is added in the example | Each extra worker adds less |
| Benefit | Gain or utility from consumption or production | Economic value/satisfaction |
| Consumer Surplus | WTP minus actual price | Buyer's extra benefit |
| Producer Surplus | Price received minus production cost | Seller's extra benefit |

# Likely Exam Questions

### Question 1

Define economics.

**Expected Answer:**

Economics is the study of how society manages its scarce resources.

### Question 2

Explain scarcity and opportunity cost.

**Expected Answer:**

Scarcity exists because resources are finite while wants are extensive. Because choices must be made, the next-best alternative given up becomes the opportunity cost.

### Question 3

What are the three basic economic decisions caused by scarcity?

**Expected Answer:**

What goods and services to produce, how to produce them, and who should receive them.

### Question 4

Differentiate movement along a demand curve and a shift in demand.

**Expected Answer:**

Movement along the curve is caused by a change in the product's own price. A demand shift is caused by a non-price determinant such as income, preferences, related-goods prices, or expectations.

### Question 5

What factors shift supply?

**Expected Answer:**

Input costs, technological improvements, the number of sellers, and government policy.

### Question 6

Define market equilibrium.

**Expected Answer:**

Market equilibrium is the price at which quantity supplied equals quantity demanded.

### Question 7

Explain surplus and shortage.

**Expected Answer:**

A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded; a shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.

### Question 8

Differentiate elastic and inelastic demand.

**Expected Answer:**

Elastic demand changes strongly when price changes, while inelastic demand remains relatively stable.

### Question 9

Differentiate fixed, variable, and marginal costs.

**Expected Answer:**

Fixed costs do not vary with output in the short run. Variable costs change with output. Marginal cost is the increase in total cost associated with additional output.

### Question 10

State the formulas for AFC, AVC, ATC, and MC.

**Expected Answer:**

```text
AFC = FC / Q
AVC = VC / Q
ATC = TC / Q
MC  = ΔTC / ΔQ
```

### Question 11

Explain the relationship between MC and ATC.

**Expected Answer:**

When MC is below ATC, ATC falls. When MC is above ATC, ATC rises. MC crosses ATC at the minimum ATC.

### Question 12

What is diminishing marginal product?

**Expected Answer:**

It is the pattern where each additional unit of an input, such as another worker in the lecture example, contributes less additional output than the previous one.

### Question 13

How can AI support production-cost decisions?

**Expected Answer:**

AI can analyze cost data, estimate total cost, monitor fixed and variable costs, predict cost changes, estimate marginal cost, identify inefficiencies, and support decisions.

### Question 14

How are consumer and producer benefits calculated?

**Expected Answer:**

```text
Consumer Benefit = Willingness to Pay - Actual Price
Producer Benefit = Price Received - Cost of Production
```

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Defining economics as only the study of money.
- Saying scarcity means no resources exist.
- Treating opportunity cost as monetary cost only.
- Forgetting that opportunity cost is the **next-best** alternative.
- Treating a virtual marketplace as not being a market.
- Confusing factor markets with product markets.
- Saying a product's own price change shifts the demand curve.
- Confusing substitutes with complements.
- Reversing the law of demand.
- Reversing the law of supply.
- Saying equilibrium means supply or demand is zero.
- Confusing surplus and shortage.
- Forgetting that Page 19's surplus at Rs 140 is `1000 - 600 = 400`.
- Forgetting that Page 19's shortage at Rs 80 is `800 - 400 = 400`.
- Defining elastic and inelastic demand backwards.
- Treating fixed cost as always zero.
- Treating marginal cost as total cost.
- Using `TC / Q` for MC.
- Forgetting that ATC is U-shaped in the lecture's example.
- Reversing the rule: `MC < ATC` means ATC is falling.
- Confusing marginal product with marginal cost.
- Reversing the consumer-surplus subtraction.
- Reversing the producer-surplus subtraction.
- Presenting supplementary activity answers as if they were printed on the original slides.
