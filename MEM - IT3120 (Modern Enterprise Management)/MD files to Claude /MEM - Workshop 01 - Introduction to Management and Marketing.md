## Page 01 — Objective, Group Formation and Presentation Requirements

### Original Page Content

**IT3120 - Modern Enterprise Management**  
**Workshop 1 – Introduction to Management and Marketing**

### Objective

The objective of this activity is to enable students to apply fundamental management and marketing concepts to real-world business situations by analyzing a contemporary organizational case study.

Through collaborative discussion and problem-solving, students will:

- Evaluate managerial and marketing challenges.
- Integrate theoretical knowledge with practical decision-making.
- Propose strategic recommendations.
- Strengthen critical thinking.
- Strengthen teamwork.
- Strengthen communication.
- Strengthen presentation skills.
- Gain exposure to a variety of industries and business contexts.

### Group Formation

- Form groups of **8 students**.
- Each group will receive **one unique case study**.
- Read the assigned case carefully.
- Each group will deliver a **10-minute presentation**, followed by a short Q&A.
- During this week's workshop, **7–8 groups** will present.
- The remaining groups will present during next week's session.

### Presentation Requirements

Each group will deliver a 10-minute presentation covering:

| Presentation Part | Allocated Time |
|---|---:|
| 1. Introduction to the case | 1 minute |
| 2. Key management analysis | 3 minutes |
| 3. Key marketing analysis | 3 minutes |
| 4. Integrated recommendations | 2 minutes |
| 5. Conclusion | 1 minute |
| **Total** | **10 minutes** |

### Visual Explanation

The page uses three clearly separated text sections: **Objective**, **Group Formation**, and **Presentation Requirements**. The numbered presentation structure represents a fixed ten-minute flow.

```text
Understand the assigned case
            ↓
Identify management challenges
            ↓
Identify marketing challenges
            ↓
Connect management + marketing concepts
            ↓
Develop justified recommendations
            ↓
Present in 10 minutes
            ↓
Answer questions
```

**Why this matters:** The workshop is not only about describing a company. Students must diagnose problems, connect theory with practice, justify recommendations, and present them collaboratively.

### Definitions

**Definition: Management analysis**  
The structured examination of how an organization plans, organizes, leads, controls resources, manages employees, and handles operations.

**Simple meaning:** Identify how the company is being managed and what management problems must be solved.

**Definition: Marketing analysis**  
The examination of customers, competition, value, positioning, communication, loyalty, and market opportunities.

**Simple meaning:** Identify what customers need, how the company creates value, and how it can attract and retain them.

**Definition: Integrated recommendation**  
A recommendation that combines management and marketing considerations rather than treating them as unrelated areas.

**Simple meaning:** Suggest an action that improves both how the company operates and how it delivers value to customers.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

A weak presentation only retells the case. A strong presentation explains:

1. What is happening.
2. Why the problem exists.
3. Which management concepts apply.
4. Which marketing concepts apply.
5. How both areas connect.
6. What the company should do.
7. Why the proposed action is practical.

### Exam Tip

The workshop structure may be assessed through presentation performance rather than a written examination. Remember the required balance:

- 1 minute: introduction
- 3 minutes: management
- 3 minutes: marketing
- 2 minutes: recommendations
- 1 minute: conclusion

### Common Mistake

Do not spend most of the ten minutes explaining company history. The presentation allocates six minutes to management and marketing analysis and only one minute to the introduction.

### Likely Workshop Questions

**Question 1:** What is the main objective of the workshop?  
**Expected answer:** To apply fundamental management and marketing concepts to a real organizational case, analyze challenges, integrate theory with practical decision-making, and propose justified strategic recommendations.

**Question 2:** Why must recommendations be integrated?  
**Expected answer:** Because operational and managerial decisions affect customer value and marketing outcomes, while marketing promises must be supported by organizational capabilities.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

The marking scheme rewards **what you think** more than **how decorative the slides look**. A high-scoring presentation must:

- Use marketing concepts accurately.
- Show the relationship between both.
- Explain why each recommendation is suitable.
- Prepare for questions.
- Demonstrate team participation.

### Exam Tip

The two highest individual criteria are **Management Analysis (20)** and **Marketing Analysis (20)**. However, the combined **Integration of Concepts** and **Critical Thinking & Justification** are worth another 30 marks.

### Common Mistake

Do not list theories without applying them to the case. Naming SWOT, leadership, segmentation, customer experience, or digital transformation earns little unless the group explains how the concept fits the specific organization.

### Likely Workshop Questions

**Question 1:** Which two individual criteria carry the highest marks?  
**Expected answer:** Management Analysis and Marketing Analysis, with 20 marks each.

**Question 2:** Why is justification essential?  
**Expected answer:** Recommendations must be supported by case evidence, relevant concepts, expected benefits, risks, and practical implementation considerations.

## Page 03 — Case Studies 1–3

### Original Page Content

### Case Study 1 — PickMe – Building a Student Super App

PickMe has successfully expanded beyond ride-hailing into food delivery, grocery delivery, parcel services, and digital payments. The company now plans to launch a lifestyle platform specifically for university students, offering learning resources, fitness services, event updates, and exclusive discounts.

While students are highly active online, they are also price-sensitive and have many competing digital platforms to choose from. PickMe must design a strategy that attracts students, delivers value, and builds long-term loyalty while managing rapid business growth.

### Case Study 2 — Dialog Axiata – Enhancing Customer Experience through Digital Transformation

Dialog Axiata has invested heavily in digital services including mobile banking, entertainment platforms, online customer service, and self-service mobile applications.

However, increasing customer expectations, growing competition, and service quality concerns require the company to continuously improve customer experience while managing operational efficiency. Dialog must balance technological innovation with personalized customer service.

### Case Study 3 — Keells – Smart Retail Innovation

Keells has introduced self-checkout counters, mobile payment options, loyalty programmes, and online grocery shopping to improve customer convenience.

While these initiatives increase efficiency, some customers still prefer traditional shopping experiences, and employees must adapt to new technologies. The company seeks to improve operational efficiency without reducing customer satisfaction or employee engagement.

### Visual Explanation

The PDF places the three case studies in vertically stacked bordered boxes. Each box contains:

1. Company and case title.
2. Current initiatives.
3. Main organizational tension.
4. Strategic challenge.

### Case Analysis Aid

> The following table organizes points already stated in the case descriptions. It is an analysis aid, not additional case information.

| Case | Management Focus | Marketing Focus | Central Tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| PickMe | Managing rapid business growth and a broader service portfolio | Student value, attraction, discounts, platform choice, loyalty | Growth and service expansion vs focused value for price-sensitive students |
| Dialog Axiata | Operational efficiency and technology-service balance | Customer experience, personalization, service quality, competition | Digital automation vs personalized human service |
| Keells | Employee adaptation, efficiency, and technology implementation | Convenience, loyalty, online shopping, customer satisfaction | Smart retail efficiency vs traditional customer preferences |

### Definitions

**Definition: Super app**  
A digital platform that combines multiple services within one application.

**Simple meaning:** One app used for several activities, such as transport, payments, food, learning, and events.

**Definition: Digital transformation**  
The use of digital technology to redesign services, operations, and customer interactions.

**Simple meaning:** Changing how a company works and serves customers by using digital systems.

**Definition: Smart retail**  
The use of digital tools, automation, and data to improve retail operations and customer convenience.

**Simple meaning:** A technology-supported supermarket or store experience.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

These cases share one pattern: technology can improve efficiency, but successful implementation depends on customer needs and employee capability.

```text
New technology
      ↓
Operational efficiency
      ↓
Customer experience changes
      ↓
Employee/customer adaptation required
      ↓
Long-term loyalty depends on delivered value
```

### Exam Tip

For each case, separate the analysis into:

- Management issue
- Marketing issue
- Link between them
- Recommendation
- Justification and risk

### Common Mistake

Do not assume that more technology automatically creates better customer experience. Dialog and Keells must balance automation with human needs, while PickMe must prove that the student platform creates enough value to overcome price sensitivity and competing apps.

### Likely Workshop Questions

**Question 1:** What common issue connects PickMe, Dialog, and Keells?  
**Expected answer:** All three use digital innovation to create customer value, but they must manage operational growth, customer expectations, and user or employee adaptation.

**Question 2:** What should be evaluated before launching PickMe’s student platform?  
**Expected answer:** Student needs, price sensitivity, competing platforms, service relevance, operational capability, value proposition, and methods for building long-term loyalty.

## Page 04 — Case Studies 4–6

### Original Page Content

### Case Study 4 — MAS Holdings – Sustainable Manufacturing for Global Markets

MAS Holdings has become an international leader in apparel manufacturing by focusing on innovation, sustainability, and ethical business practices.

Increasing global demand for environmentally friendly products requires MAS to continuously invest in green technologies while maintaining productivity, controlling costs, and motivating a diverse workforce operating across multiple countries.

### Case Study 5 — Daraz Sri Lanka – Improving E-Commerce Customer Experience

As one of Sri Lanka's largest online marketplaces, Daraz serves millions of customers with thousands of sellers.

Customer complaints regarding delayed deliveries, inconsistent product quality, and after-sales service have affected customer satisfaction. Daraz must strengthen seller management, improve logistics, and build customer trust while maintaining rapid business growth.

### Case Study 6 — Cargills Food City – Expanding Omnichannel Retail

Cargills has expanded its online ordering and home delivery services alongside its physical supermarkets.

Customers now expect seamless shopping experiences across mobile apps, websites, and physical stores. The company must integrate digital technologies, improve inventory management, and deliver consistent customer experiences while remaining competitive in the retail market.

### Visual Explanation

The page uses three bordered case-study blocks. Each case combines an operational challenge with a market or customer expectation:

```text
MAS
Green investment + global workforce + cost/productivity
                         ↓
Sustainable value for global markets

Daraz
Seller control + logistics + after-sales service
                         ↓
Trust and customer satisfaction

Cargills

                         ↓

```

### Case Analysis Aid

| Case | Management Focus | Marketing Focus | Central Tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| MAS Holdings | Green investment, productivity, cost control, diverse global workforce | Demand for sustainable and ethical products | Sustainability investment vs productivity and cost |
| Daraz Sri Lanka | Seller management, logistics, after-sales systems, rapid growth | Trust, satisfaction, consistent product quality | Marketplace scale vs consistent service quality |
| Cargills Food City | Technology integration and inventory management | Seamless experience across app, web, delivery, and stores | Multiple channels vs one consistent customer experience |

### Definitions

**Definition: Sustainable manufacturing**  
Manufacturing that reduces environmental harm while maintaining business and operational performance.

**Simple meaning:** Producing goods responsibly without ignoring cost, quality, employees, or productivity.

**Definition: E-commerce marketplace**  
A digital platform where many independent sellers offer products to customers.

**Simple meaning:** An online shopping platform that connects buyers with many sellers.

**Definition: Omnichannel retail**  
A retail approach that connects physical and digital channels into a consistent customer experience.

**Simple meaning:** Customers should receive one smooth experience whether they shop in-store, through an app, on a website, or by delivery.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

MAS must make environmental responsibility operationally sustainable. Daraz must control the quality of a marketplace it does not fully operate as a single seller. Cargills must connect separate channels so that prices, inventory, service, and customer information remain consistent.

### Exam Tip

Use the correct central concept:

- MAS: sustainability and global operations
- Daraz: service quality, logistics, seller governance, trust
- Cargills: omnichannel integration and inventory accuracy

### Common Mistake

Do not confuse **multichannel** with **omnichannel**. Having stores, an app, and a website is multichannel. Integrating them into one seamless experience is omnichannel.

### Likely Workshop Questions

**Question 1:** Why is seller management a management and marketing issue for Daraz?  
**Expected answer:** Seller controls affect operations and quality, while product consistency, delivery, and after-sales service determine customer trust and satisfaction.

**Question 2:** What must Cargills integrate to provide an omnichannel experience?  
**Expected answer:** Mobile apps, websites, physical stores, delivery services, inventory information, and service standards.

Netflix faces increasing competition from global streaming services offering exclusive content and lower subscription prices.

### Case Analysis Aid

### Definitions

**Definition: Service recovery**  
Actions taken to correct service failures and restore customer confidence.

**Simple meaning:** Fix the problem, communicate properly, and rebuild trust after customers receive poor service.

**Definition: Brand reputation**  
The overall perception stakeholders hold about an organization based on its actions, performance, and communication.

**Simple meaning:** What people believe and feel about the brand.

**Definition: Lean Manufacturing**  
An operational philosophy focused on eliminating waste, improving flow, and continuously improving processes.

**Simple meaning:** Create more value with less waste.

**Definition: Customer retention**  
The ability of a business to keep existing customers over time.

**Simple meaning:** Prevent customers from leaving for competitors.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

These companies cannot solve marketing problems with communication alone. For example, SriLankan Airlines cannot reposition itself as reliable unless operations actually become more reliable. Cinnamon’s digital personalization must be supported by employee service. Toyota’s innovation must preserve operational discipline. Netflix’s personalization must support content value rather than only collecting more data.

### Exam Tip

A strong recommendation must connect internal capability to the external promise:

```text
Internal operations + employees + technology
                    ↓
Actual service/product quality
                    ↓
Customer experience
                    ↓
Brand reputation, loyalty, or retention
```

### Common Mistake

Do not recommend “more advertising” when the root cause is operational. Promotion can increase expectations, but it cannot repair flight delays, inconsistent service, supply chain problems, or weak content value.

### Likely Workshop Questions

**Question 1:** Why is employee performance important to Cinnamon’s marketing?  
**Expected answer:** Hospitality experiences are delivered by employees, so personalized branding and loyalty promises depend on consistent service performance.

**Question 2:** How can Toyota maintain Lean efficiency while innovating?  
**Expected answer:** Introduce technology through continuous improvement, employee training, supply chain coordination, controlled experimentation, and performance measurement.

# Full Workshop Summary

Workshop 01 requires groups of eight students to analyze one contemporary organizational case and deliver a ten-minute presentation followed by Q&A. The presentation must include a one-minute introduction, three minutes of management analysis, three minutes of marketing analysis, two minutes of integrated recommendations, and a one-minute conclusion.

The evaluation gives 40 marks to management and marketing analysis, 30 marks to integration and critical thinking, and 30 marks to content, Q&A, presentation skills, and team participation. Therefore, the strongest strategy is to focus on evidence-based analysis and justified recommendations rather than spending excessive time on company background or slide decoration.

The sixteen cases cover recurring enterprise themes:

- Digital transformation
- Customer experience
- Service quality
- Operational efficiency
- Employee engagement
- Sustainability
- Trust
- Brand reputation
- Innovation
- Growth and scalability
- Customer loyalty and retention
- Supply chain management
- Regulation
- Data and artificial intelligence
- Local and global market adaptation
- Omnichannel integration

A consistent analysis method can be used for every case:

```text
1. Identify the organization’s goal.
2. Extract the management challenges.
3. Extract the marketing challenges.
4. Identify affected stakeholders.
5. Select relevant concepts.
6. Explain how management and marketing issues interact.
7. Generate realistic recommendations.
8. Justify expected benefits.
9. Identify implementation risks.
```

# Likely Exam and Presentation Questions

## Question 1
What is the difference between management analysis and marketing analysis?

**Expected Answer:**  
Management analysis examines organizational resources, people, operations, leadership, planning, and control. Marketing analysis examines customers, competition, value, positioning, experience, loyalty, and market strategy.

## Question 2
What makes a recommendation “integrated”?

**Expected Answer:**  
It connects internal organizational capability with external customer and market value. For example, a digital customer promise must be supported by employee training, systems, processes, and service quality.

## Question 3
Why can digital transformation fail to improve customer experience?

**Expected Answer:**  
Technology may be poorly integrated, employees may not be trained, systems may be unreliable, service may become impersonal, or the solution may not match customer needs.

## Question 4
Why is employee engagement relevant to marketing?

**Expected Answer:**  
Employees deliver the service and influence quality, trust, satisfaction, reputation, loyalty, and the actual customer experience.

## Question 5
How should a group justify a strategic recommendation?

**Expected Answer:**  
Use case evidence, relevant theory, expected benefits, implementation steps, resource needs, risks, stakeholder impact, and measurable outcomes.

## Question 6
Why is operational performance essential to brand reputation?

**Expected Answer:**  
Customers judge the brand through actual delivery. Advertising cannot sustain a reputation that contradicts delays, poor quality, weak service, or unreliable operations.

## Question 7
What is the key difference between multichannel and omnichannel retail?

**Expected Answer:**  
Multichannel means the organization uses several channels. Omnichannel means those channels are integrated into one consistent and seamless customer experience.

## Question 8
What should be included in Q&A preparation?

**Expected Answer:**  
Evidence for recommendations, alternatives considered, expected cost and benefits, risks, implementation responsibilities, stakeholder impact, and measures of success.
