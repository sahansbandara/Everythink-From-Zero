# MEM – Workshop 02 | Case Study 8: Lack of Skills Development
**Target Q&A Pack (20 Questions) — Viva / Exam Ready**

---

## 📌 Case Summary (memorise this frame)

> An IT company adopted **cloud computing** and **AI technologies**, but many employees **lack the required technical skills**. **Limited training opportunities** left employees **overwhelmed**, resulting in **lower productivity** and **project delays**.

**Core HRM area:** Learning & Development (Training and Development)
**Secondary areas:** Resourcing Strategy, Change Management, Performance Management, Motivation

```text
Technology adopted → Skills NOT planned → Skill gap
      → Limited training → Employees overwhelmed
            → Low productivity + Project delays
```

🧠 **Trick:** **T-S-T-O-P** → Technology → Skill gap → Training absent → Overwhelmed → Productivity down.

---

## 🔴 SECTION A — Problem Identification (Q1–Q6)

### Q1. What is the main HRM problem in this case?
> The main problem is a **lack of employee Learning and Development (L&D)**. The company adopted new technologies (cloud, AI) **without a corresponding skills development plan**, creating a **skills gap** between the technology requirement and the existing workforce capability.

⚫ **Trap:** Do NOT say "the problem is new technology." Technology is the *trigger*, not the problem. The problem is the **HR failure to plan for skills**.

---

### Q2. What is a "skills gap"? Define it.
> A **skills gap** is the difference between the **skills an organization requires** to achieve its objectives and the **skills its current employees actually possess**.

---

### Q3. Why did this skills gap occur? (Root cause)
> Because the company treated technology adoption as a **technical decision, not a people decision**. There was **no workforce planning or job analysis** before adoption, so training needs were never identified, and only **limited training opportunities** were provided afterwards.

✍️ **Keyword:** *"Technology strategy was not aligned with HR resourcing strategy."*

---

### Q4. What are the direct consequences stated in the case?
| Consequence | Type |
|---|---|
| Employees feel **overwhelmed** | Psychological / well-being |
| **Lower productivity** | Operational |
| **Project delays** | Business / client-facing |

---

### Q5. What are the *hidden* consequences not stated in the case?
> **Increased turnover** (employees leave when they feel incapable), **higher stress and burnout**, **lower quality output / technical debt**, **loss of client trust**, and **increased dependency on a few skilled employees** (key-person risk).

---

### Q6. Which HRM function has failed here, and which functions are indirectly affected?
| Function | Status |
|---|---|
| **Learning & Development** | ❌ Primary failure |
| **Resourcing Strategy** | ❌ No workforce planning before adoption |
| Performance Management | ⚠ Affected — targets unachievable without skills |
| Motivation & Engagement | ⚠ Affected — employees overwhelmed, confidence drops |
| Employee Well-being | ⚠ Affected — stress, pressure |

---

## 🟠 SECTION B — Theory Application (Q7–Q13)

### Q7. Define Learning & Development as an HRM function.
> **Learning & Development** is the HRM function of providing **training and development opportunities to enhance employee capabilities and support career growth**.

⚫ **Trap:** Training ≠ Development.
- **Training** = short-term, job-specific, current role (e.g. AWS certification).
- **Development** = long-term, career-oriented, future roles (e.g. cloud architect path).

---

### Q8. Why is employee development *especially* critical in the IT industry?
> Because **technology evolves rapidly**, so constant **upskilling** is required. In IT, the organization's main asset is the **knowledge and skill of its people**, so if skills become outdated, the organization's capability becomes outdated immediately.

---

### Q9. Apply the Training Needs Analysis (TNA) idea to this case.
```text
Organizational level → Cloud + AI capability required
Task level          → Which roles need which specific skills?
Individual level    → Who has the skill, who has the gap?
```
> The company skipped all three levels. A **TNA** should have been conducted **before** technology adoption to identify who needed which training.

---

### Q10. Link this case to **motivation theory**.
> Under **Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory**, **growth, achievement, and advancement** are **motivators**. Removing training opportunities removes motivators, so employees lose engagement — not just capability.
> Under **Maslow**, employees are stuck at the **safety/esteem** level (fear of failure, low competence confidence) and cannot reach **self-actualization**.

⚫ **Trap (your recurring one):** Don't just name Herzberg. State **which factor and why** — "training is a *motivator*, not a hygiene factor, because it drives growth, not just dissatisfaction removal."

---

### Q11. Link this case to **change management**.
> Adopting cloud and AI is an **organizational change**. HRM's role in change is to **help employees adapt, communicate the change, and align people with the new strategy**. The company implemented the technical change but **not the people-side change**, which is why employees feel overwhelmed rather than resistant.

---

### Q12. How does this case affect **performance management**?
> Performance management requires **realistic, achievable goals**. Here employees are measured against outputs that require skills they do not have, so appraisals become **unfair** and demotivating. Performance management must therefore include a **development component**, not only monitoring.

---

### Q13. What is the relationship between this case and **retention**?
> Skilled IT workers are **highly mobile**. Employees who feel unsupported and unable to grow will leave for employers offering learning opportunities — so a training failure quickly becomes a **retention failure** and increases recruitment cost.

---

## 🟢 SECTION C — Solutions & Recommendations (Q14–Q18)

### Q14. Give at least three practical HRM solutions. *(Workshop requirement)*
| # | Solution | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Conduct a Training Needs Analysis (TNA)** | Identifies the exact gap per role instead of generic training |
| 2 | **Structured upskilling programme + certifications** (AWS/Azure, AI fundamentals) | Builds verified, job-relevant capability |
| 3 | **Allocate paid learning time** (e.g. dedicated learning hours per week) | Removes the "no time to learn" barrier |
| 4 | **Mentoring / internal knowledge sharing** by skilled seniors | Low-cost, fast, retains knowledge in-house |
| 5 | **Phased technology rollout** | Reduces overwhelm; skills grow with adoption |
| 6 | **Link L&D to career development plans** | Turns training into a motivator and retention tool |

---

### Q15. What is the **trade-off** of your recommended solution?
> Training has a **short-term cost**: money, and **productive hours lost** while employees learn. There is also **retention risk** — trained employees become more marketable and may leave.
> **Counter:** the cost of *not* training is higher — continued delays, quality issues, and turnover. Mitigate the retention risk by pairing training with **career paths and recognition**.

✍️ This trade-off answer is where the marks are. Always give it.

---

### Q16. Should the company hire new skilled staff instead of training existing ones?
> **Hiring is not a substitute.** It is **expensive, slow, and damages morale** of existing employees. The best approach is a **hybrid**: hire a small number of experienced cloud/AI specialists to act as **internal mentors and champions**, while upskilling the existing workforce. This solves the immediate delivery gap *and* the long-term capability gap.

---

### Q17. How would you measure whether the solution worked? (KPIs)
```text
Certifications completed  →  Training hours per employee
Productivity / velocity   →  Project delay rate ↓
Employee engagement score →  Voluntary turnover ↓
```

---

### Q18. What is the IT-industry relevance of this case? *(Presentation requirement)*
> In IT, **human capital is the product capability**. Cloud and AI adoption is now standard across the industry, so organizations that fail at continuous upskilling lose **innovation capacity, delivery speed, and competitiveness**. HRM is therefore a **strategic**, not administrative, function in IT.

---

## ⚫ SECTION D — Trap / Follow-up Questions (Q19–Q20)

### Q19. "Isn't this just a management problem, not an HRM problem?"
> It is both, but it is **fundamentally HRM**. Management made the adoption decision, but the failure point is **resourcing strategy and learning & development** — identifying, planning, and building workforce capability. That is HRM's defined responsibility.

---

### Q20. "The employees are overwhelmed — is that a motivation problem or a skills problem?"
> **Primarily a skills problem that has produced a motivation symptom.** Employees are not unwilling; they are **unable**. Giving rewards or recognition alone would not fix it, because the root cause is **capability, not effort**. Fix capability first; motivation recovers as competence returns.

⚫ **Trap:** Never answer "motivation problem." That misdiagnoses the case and loses the mark.
