### Slide 04 — What is an Organization?

#### Original Slide Content

The word **organization** comes from:

- Medieval Latin *organizationem* = the act of organizing.
- Latin *organum* = “instrument, tool, organ.”
- Greek word *organon* = “tool, instrument, implement.”

The historical meanings evolved as follows:

- **Early 15th century:** referred to the structure of a body or its organs.
- **Mid-15th century:** the act or process of arranging parts into a functioning whole.
- **Later, during the 18th–19th centuries:** a formally structured group of people working together toward common objectives.

#### Visual Explanation

The slide contains a picture of a pipe organ. It visually connects the modern word **organization** with the older root *organon/organum*, meaning an instrument or a set of parts arranged to function together.

#### Definition

**Definition:** An organization is a formally arranged group of people and resources that work together toward common objectives.

**Simple meaning:** Different people and parts are coordinated so that they can achieve a shared goal.

#### Visual/Text Diagram

```text
Individual people and resources
              ↓
Parts are arranged and coordinated
              ↓
They function as a connected whole
              ↓
Common objectives are achieved
```

#### Exam Tip

For a definition question, include these keywords: **people**, **structure**, **coordination**, **resources**, and **common objectives**.

#### Common Mistake

An organization is not merely a building or a collection of people. It requires an arrangement of roles, relationships, resources, and shared objectives.

#### Likely Exam Questions

1. **What is an organization?**  
   **Expected answer:** A formally structured group of people and resources coordinated to achieve common objectives.

2. **How did the meaning of “organization” evolve?**  
   **Expected answer:** It developed from the idea of tools or body organs, to arranging parts into a functioning whole, and later to a structured group working toward shared goals.

### Slide 05 — What is an Organization?

#### Original Slide Content

> “Organization is the process of identifying and grouping work to be performed, defining and delegating responsibility and authority and establishing relationships for the purpose of enabling people to work most effectively together in accomplishing objectives.”  
> — **Louis Allen**

#### Explanation

Louis Allen’s definition emphasizes organization as a **process**, not only as an institution. The process includes:

1. Identifying the work that must be done.
2. Grouping related work.
3. Defining responsibilities.
4. Delegating authority.
5. Establishing working relationships.
6. Enabling people to achieve objectives effectively.

#### Visual/Text Diagram

```text
Identify work

Group related activities
     ↓
Assign responsibility and authority
     ↓
Establish working relationships
     ↓
People work effectively toward objectives
```

### Slide 08 — What is Management?

#### Original Slide Content

**Henri Fayol (1841–1925):**

> “Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling the efforts of organization members and of using all other organizational resources to achieve stated organizational goals.”

**Mary Parker Follett (1868–1933):**

> Management is “the art of getting things done through people.”

She described management as a philosophy.

#### Definition

**Definition:** Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling people and resources to achieve organizational goals.

**Simple meaning:** Managers decide what must be achieved, arrange the work, guide people, and check whether the required results are being produced.

#### Comparison Table

| Thinker | Main idea | Key emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Henri Fayol | Management is a process involving planning, organizing, leading, and controlling | Functions and resources |
| Mary Parker Follett | Management is the art of getting things done through people | Cooperation and people |

#### Exam Tip

Remember both viewpoints. Fayol presents management as a **structured process**, while Follett emphasizes accomplishing work **through people**.

#### Common Mistake

Management does not mean personally completing every task. Managers achieve results by coordinating people and organizational resources.

#### Likely Exam Questions

1. **Define management according to Henri Fayol.**  
   **Expected answer:** It is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling members and other resources to achieve stated organizational goals.

2. **What is Mary Parker Follett’s view of management?**  
   **Expected answer:** It is the art of getting things done through people.

## Page 06 — Four Management Functions

### Slide 12 — Management Functions

#### Original Slide Content

Management can be understood through four key functions:

1. Planning
2. Organizing
3. Leading
4. Controlling

Together, these four functions provide a structured framework for understanding how managers coordinate people and resources effectively within organizations.

#### Visual Explanation

The slide uses four icons:

- A checklist and clock for **Planning**.
- A structured list for **Organizing**.
- A person guiding another upward for **Leading**.
- People, gears, and a supporting hand for **Controlling**.

The icons show that management covers future preparation, work arrangement, human guidance, and performance monitoring.

---

### Slide 13 — Management Functions Explained

#### Original Slide Content

| Function | Lecture explanation |
|---|---|
| Planning | Setting organizational goals and determining the best course of action. Managers identify objectives, develop strategies, and allocate resources in advance to guide future activities. |
| Organizing | Arranging resources and tasks in a structured way, including assigning responsibilities, grouping activities, and establishing relationships among team members to ensure efficient workflow. |
| Leading | Guiding, motivating, and influencing employees to work toward organizational goals. It includes communication, leadership, and creating a positive work environment to enhance performance. |
| Controlling | Monitoring performance and ensuring that activities align with planned objectives. Managers compare actual outcomes with expected results and take corrective actions when necessary. |

#### Visual/Text Diagram

```text
Planning → Organizing → Leading → Controlling
    ↑                                  ↓
    └──── feedback and adjustment ─────┘
```

The relationship is continuous rather than a rigid one-time sequence.

#### Exam Tip

For each function, state both the **main action** and its **purpose**:

- Planning — set goals and decide actions.
- Organizing — arrange tasks and resources.
- Leading — guide and motivate people.
- Controlling — compare results and correct deviations.

#### Common Mistake

Do not confuse **organizing** with **planning**. Planning decides *what and how to achieve goals*; organizing arranges *who, what resources, and what relationships* will execute the plan.

#### Likely Exam Questions

1. **Name and explain the four management functions.**  
   **Expected answer:** Planning, organizing, leading, and controlling, with a short explanation of each.

2. **Why are the four functions connected?**  
   **Expected answer:** Results and feedback from one function influence the others, allowing managers to coordinate and adjust organizational work.

## Page 07 — Management as a Process and Planning

### Slide 14 — Introduction to Functions of Management

#### Original Slide Content

- **Processes**
  - Ongoing activities that are interrelated.
- **Ongoing**
  - Activities are not completed in a strictly linear, step-by-step fashion.
  - Some activities continue while others begin.
- **Interrelated**
  - The results of tasks influence each other.
  - Tasks must be performed efficiently.

**Important:** Processes do not always go in order.

#### Visual Explanation

A meeting image shows people seated around a circular table. This reinforces that management involves continuous coordination and interaction rather than isolated, one-directional tasks.

#### Explanation

Managers may plan, organize, lead, and control at the same time. For example, while leading a team, a manager may monitor performance and revise the plan.

---

### Slide 15 — 1 Planning

#### Original Slide Content

- Defining goals and tackling them.
- Defines the future of the organization through long-term plans.
- Develops **strategic plans**:
  - Long-term.
  - Affect the entire organization.
  - Bridge the gap between what the organization is and what it wants to be.
- Develops **tactical plans**:
  - Translate strategic plans into specific actions.
  - Identify who, what, where, and related implementation details.

#### Visual Explanation

The slide includes a collection of clocks, representing future orientation, deadlines, scheduling, and the time dimension of planning.

#### Comparison Table

| Plan type | Scope | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic plan | Long-term and organization-wide | Defines the future direction and closes the gap between current and desired states |
| Tactical plan | More specific and action-oriented | Converts strategy into details such as who, what, and where |

#### Definition

**Definition:** Planning is the management function of defining goals and deciding the courses of action needed to achieve them.

**Simple meaning:** Decide where the organization wants to go and how it will get there.

#### Exam Tip

The phrase **“bridges the gap between what the organization is and what it wants to be”** is a strong keyword for explaining strategic planning.

#### Common Mistake

Do not treat strategic and tactical plans as identical. Strategic plans establish broad long-term direction; tactical plans translate that direction into specific actions.

## Page 08 — Organizing and Leading

### Slide 16 — 2 Organizing

#### Original Slide Content

- Decides how best to implement plans.
- Decides how an organization is structured.
- Assigns authority and responsibility.
- Works to acquire resources.
- Decides coordination.

#### Visual Explanation

The desk image contains a laptop, planner, calendar, documents, and other work tools. It represents arranging resources, schedules, tasks, and responsibilities in an orderly way.

#### Definition

**Definition:** Organizing is the process of arranging tasks, people, authority, responsibility, coordination, and resources so that plans can be implemented.

**Simple meaning:** Put the right people and resources in the right arrangement to perform the plan.

---

### Slide 17 — 3 Leading

#### Original Slide Content

- Uses knowledge, character, and charisma to inspire achievement of goals.
- Leads through:
  - Communication
  - Building commitment
  - Creating shared values
  - Encouraging high performance
- Uses the power of granting rewards and punishments.

#### Visual Explanation

The image shows one person standing in front of a group with a raised hand. It represents influence, direction, visibility, and a leader guiding others.

#### Definition

**Definition:** Leading is the management function of guiding, influencing, communicating with, and motivating people to achieve organizational goals.

**Simple meaning:** Help people understand the goal, commit to it, and perform well.

#### Comparison Table

| Organizing | Leading |
|---|---|
| Arranges structure, resources, roles, and authority | Influences people’s behavior and commitment |
| Focuses on work design and coordination | Focuses on communication, motivation, values, and performance |

#### Exam Tip

When explaining leading, include **communication**, **motivation**, **commitment**, **shared values**, and **influence**.

#### Common Mistake

Assigning authority is mainly part of organizing. Inspiring and influencing employees to use that structure effectively is part of leading.

#### Likely Exam Question

**Differentiate organizing and leading.**  
**Expected answer:** Organizing arranges tasks, authority, people, and resources; leading guides and motivates those people to work toward goals.

### Slide 18 — 4 Controlling

#### Original Slide Content

- Unexpected issues will arise.
- Controlling is the process of:
  - Monitoring activities.
  - Measuring performance.
  - Comparing results.
  - Making corrections.
- It involves observing and responding to what happens.
- **The feedback loop:**
  - The most important aspect of controlling is that managers must remain informed.

#### Visual Explanation

The circular feedback graphic contains four connected ideas:

1. Ask customers whether they like the new product.
2. Assess progress: is it selling?
3. Sell the improved product.
4. Fix it, improve it, and make changes.

The loop then repeats. This shows that controlling is continuous: information is collected, results are assessed, and corrective changes are made.

#### Visual/Text Diagram

```text
Set or use expected results
          ↓
Monitor actual activities
          ↓
Measure and compare performance
          ↓
Identify deviations or problems
          ↓
Take corrective action
          ↓
Receive new feedback and repeat
```

#### Definition

**Definition:** Controlling is monitoring and measuring performance, comparing actual results with expected results, and correcting deviations.

**Simple meaning:** Check whether the plan is working and fix problems when it is not.

### Slide 19 — Who Directs Each Function of Management?

#### Original Slide Content

- Leaders often step up during crises that require immediate action.
- All managers perform each management function at different times; the amount depends on their position.
- Different activities may happen at the same time in an organization.

#### Visual Explanation

Four circles—Planning, Organizing, Leading, and Controlling—are connected by arrows in several directions. The diagram shows that every function can influence every other function.

    ↕          ↕

Do not describe the functions as a strict sequence that ends after controlling. Feedback often causes new planning and reorganizing.

### Slide 27 — Class Discussion: Managers and Leadership

#### Original Slide Content

**Question:** Which type of manager spends more time in leadership activities?

#### Expected Discussion

All managers perform leadership activities, but managers and team leaders who work closest to employees often spend a larger proportion of their time communicating, guiding, supporting, and resolving immediate people-related issues. The exact balance depends on the organization and position.

When answering a discussion question, avoid giving only a title. Explain the answer using **employee contact**, **communication**, **guidance**, and **operational responsibility**.

#### Common Mistake

Do not claim that only top managers lead. Leadership is required at every managerial level.

## Page 14 — Qualities of a Good Manager and Activity

### Slide 28 — What Makes a Good Manager?

#### Original Slide Content

A good manager demonstrates:

- Effectiveness
- Efficiency
- Sustainable practices
- Competitive advantage
  - The business outperforms rivals due to employees.
- Dependence on a stable and reliable workforce.

#### Visual Explanation

The meeting image shows a manager communicating with colleagues. It reinforces that managerial quality is demonstrated through coordinated people, decisions, and workplace relationships.

#### Definitions

**Effectiveness:** Achieving the desired organizational goals.

**Simple meaning:** Doing the right things and reaching the required result.

**Efficiency:** Using resources carefully and minimizing waste while completing work.

**Simple meaning:** Achieving results with appropriate use of time, money, people, and materials.

**Competitive advantage:** An organizational strength that allows a business to outperform rivals.

**Simple meaning:** A reason customers, employees, or markets prefer one organization over competing organizations.

#### Comparison Table

| Concept | Main question |
|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Did we achieve the intended goal? |
| Efficiency | Did we use resources well while achieving it? |
| Sustainability | Can the practices continue responsibly over time? |
| Competitive advantage | Does this help the organization outperform rivals? |

#### Exam Tip

A strong manager should pursue both **effectiveness** and **efficiency**. One without the other may produce the wrong result or waste resources.

#### Common Mistake

Do not define effectiveness as “working quickly” or “using fewer resources.” Those ideas are more closely related to efficiency.

# Full Lecture Summary

The lecture begins by defining an **organization** as a formally arranged group of people and resources working toward common objectives. Louis Allen’s definition emphasizes the process of identifying and grouping work, delegating responsibility and authority, and establishing relationships. SLIIT is used as an example containing people, goals, structure, resources, rules and processes, and leadership.

**Management** is then presented through Henri Fayol’s functional definition and Mary Parker Follett’s people-centered definition. Management has both a people aspect and a process aspect. Its four main functions are planning, organizing, leading, and controlling. These functions are ongoing, interrelated, and may occur simultaneously.

- **Planning** defines goals, long-term strategic direction, and tactical actions.
- **Organizing** arranges structure, responsibilities, authority, resources, and coordination.
- **Leading** communicates, inspires, motivates, builds commitment, and encourages performance.
- **Controlling** monitors activities, measures and compares results, receives feedback, and takes corrective action.

Management exists at different levels. **Top managers** focus on long-term success, the external environment, goals, and major investments. **Middle managers** link strategic direction with operational implementation. **First-line managers** work closely with employees and daily operations. **Team leaders** coordinate timelines, assignments, training, instructions, and team efficiency. Good managers balance effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability, competitive advantage, and workforce stability.

**Organizational behavior** studies how people act, interact, and perform at individual, team, and organizational levels. The lecture presents scientific management, human relations, contingency, systems, cultural, critical, and symbolic perspectives. It also compares classical, behavioral, quantitative, and modern management approaches.

Organizations may adopt **functional, divisional, matrix, flat, tall, centralized, or decentralized structures**. Each provides different advantages and limitations related to specialization, independence, reporting relationships, supervision, communication, flexibility, consistency, and employee involvement.

**Power** is the capacity to influence decisions, behaviors, work, resources, and outcomes. It may arise from formal position, rewards, punishment, expertise, charisma, information, or influential connections.

**Motivation** consists of internal drives and external influences that initiate, direct, and sustain behavior. It affects direction, effort, and persistence. Maslow arranges needs from physiological requirements to self-actualization. Herzberg separates hygiene factors that prevent dissatisfaction from motivators that create satisfaction. Intrinsic motivation comes from enjoyment and internal satisfaction, whereas extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards or pressure.

The Google case integrates the lecture. Employee autonomy supports intrinsic motivation, managers operate as coaches, open communication strengthens teamwork, and an innovative culture encourages experimentation and learning from failure.

# Likely Exam Questions and Short Answers

### Question 1

**Define an organization and state its main components.**

**Expected Answer:** An organization is a structured group of people and resources coordinated to achieve common objectives. Its components include people, goals, structure, resources, rules and processes, and leadership.

### Question 2

**Compare Henri Fayol’s and Mary Parker Follett’s views of management.**

**Expected Answer:** Fayol defines management as planning, organizing, leading, and controlling resources to achieve goals. Follett describes it as getting things done through people.

### Question 3

**Explain the four functions of management.**

**Expected Answer:** Planning sets goals and actions; organizing arranges work and resources; leading influences and motivates employees; controlling monitors results and corrects deviations.

### Question 4

**Differentiate strategic and tactical planning.**

**Expected Answer:** Strategic planning defines long-term organization-wide direction. Tactical planning translates strategy into specific actions, responsibilities, locations, and implementation details.

### Question 5

**Compare top, middle, and first-line managers.**

**Expected Answer:** Top managers set long-term direction and respond to external conditions. Middle managers translate strategy into departmental objectives. First-line managers supervise daily operations and work closely with employees.

### Question 6

**Differentiate effectiveness and efficiency.**

**Expected Answer:** Effectiveness means achieving the intended goal; efficiency means using resources with minimum waste while performing work.

### Question 7

**Explain the three levels of organizational behavior.**

**Expected Answer:** The individual level studies one employee’s psychology and behavior; the group level studies teamwork and interaction; the organizational level studies structures and systems across the entire organization.

### Question 8

**Compare the classical, behavioral, quantitative, and modern approaches.**

**Expected Answer:** Classical emphasizes rules and structure; behavioral emphasizes people and motivation; quantitative uses data and mathematical models; modern emphasizes flexibility, systems, contingency, culture, and environment.

### Question 9

**Compare functional and divisional structures.**

**Expected Answer:** Functional structures group employees by specialization and improve expertise but can create departmental barriers. Divisional structures group by product, service, or region and improve focus but may duplicate resources.

### Question 10

**Explain matrix, flat, and tall structures.**

**Expected Answer:** Matrix combines functional and divisional reporting; flat has few levels and faster communication; tall has many levels and strong supervision but slower decisions.

### Question 11

**Name and explain five types of power.**

**Expected Answer:** Any five from legitimate, reward, corrective, expert, referent, information, and connection power, with the source of influence clearly explained.

### Question 12

**What are the three key aspects of motivation?**

**Expected Answer:** Direction of behavior, level of effort, and persistence.

### Question 13

**Explain Maslow’s hierarchy using workplace examples.**

**Expected Answer:** Physiological needs relate to salary and rest; safety to job security; social to teamwork; esteem to recognition; self-actualization to growth and creativity.

### Question 14

**Differentiate Herzberg’s hygiene factors and motivators.**

**Expected Answer:** Hygiene factors such as salary and conditions prevent dissatisfaction. Motivators such as achievement and responsibility create satisfaction and stronger performance.

### Question 15

**Differentiate intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.**

**Expected Answer:** Intrinsic motivation comes from enjoyment or personal satisfaction in the task. Extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards or avoiding punishment.

### Question 16

**Apply organizational behavior concepts to the Google case.**

**Expected Answer:** Autonomy increases intrinsic motivation; coaching leadership empowers employees; innovative culture shapes experimentation; open communication strengthens teamwork and collaboration.

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Treating an organization as only a group of people without goals, structure, processes, or resources.
- Describing management as personally performing every task rather than coordinating work through people.
- Mixing planning with organizing.
- Describing the four management functions as a rigid sequence with no feedback.
- Confusing effectiveness with efficiency.
- Reversing top, middle, and first-line managerial responsibilities.
- Saying the contingency approach provides one universal method.
- Confusing functional grouping with divisional grouping.
- Assuming flat organizations have no managers.
- Confusing expert power with information power.
- Treating motivation as only salary or bonuses.
- Reversing Maslow’s hierarchy.
- Saying hygiene factors strongly motivate employees under Herzberg’s theory.
- Confusing intrinsic enjoyment with extrinsic reward.
- Repeating case facts without linking them to management concepts.
