## Page 01 - Objective and Presentation Requirements

### Original Page Content

**IT3120 - Modern Enterprise Management**  
**Workshop 2**  
**Introduction to Human Resource Management**

### Objective

The objective of this activity is to enable students to apply **Human Resource Management (HRM)** concepts to real-world IT industry scenarios by:

- Analyzing contemporary organizational challenges.
- Proposing practical, theory-based solutions.
- Using collaborative learning.
- Strengthening analytical thinking.
- Strengthening problem-solving.
- Strengthening teamwork.
- Strengthening communication.
- Strengthening presentation skills.
- Demonstrating the relevance of HRM practices in achieving organizational success.

### Presentation Requirements

Each group should prepare a **10-minute presentation** of approximately **6-8 slides** covering the following:

1. **Introduction**
   - Brief overview of the organization and the HRM issue.

2. **Problem Identification**
   - Clearly identify the key HRM problem(s).
   - Explain the impact on employees and organizational performance.

3. **Analysis**
   - Analyze the root causes using relevant HRM concepts and theories.
   - Identify the HR functions involved.

4. **Recommendations**
   - Propose at least **three practical HRM solutions**.
   - Justify recommendations using appropriate theories and best practices.

5. **IT Industry Relevance**
   - Explain how the proposed solutions would improve:
     - Project performance.
     - Employee satisfaction.
     - Organizational effectiveness.

6. **Conclusion**
   - Summarize the key findings and recommendations.

### Visual Explanation

The page is structured as a workshop brief. The upper section defines the objective, and the lower section provides a six-part presentation sequence.

```text
Introduction
    |
    v
Identify the HRM problem
    |
    v
Analyze root causes + HR functions
    |
    v
Propose at least 3 practical solutions
    |
    v
Explain IT-industry impact
    |
    v
Conclusion
```

**Why this matters:** The presentation must move from diagnosis to analysis and then to justified action. It should not stop at describing the problem.

### Supplementary Definition

**Definition: Human Resource Management (HRM)**  
The management of people-related activities that support organizational goals.

**Simple meaning:** HRM is how an organization attracts, develops, supports, motivates, manages, and retains employees.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

A high-quality presentation should answer five questions:

1. What is the HR problem?
2. Why is it happening?
3. Which HR functions are involved?
4. What should the organization do?
5. How will those solutions improve employees and project/business performance?

### Exam Tip

Remember the required presentation logic:

**Problem -> Root cause -> HR function -> Recommendation -> IT-industry impact**

### Common Mistake

Do not jump directly from the case description to recommendations. The PDF explicitly requires root-cause analysis using HRM concepts and theories.

### Likely Exam / Presentation Questions

**Question 1:** What is the main objective of Workshop 2?  
**Expected answer:** To apply HRM concepts to real-world IT-industry challenges and propose practical, theory-based solutions.

**Question 2:** How many practical HRM solutions should each group propose?  
**Expected answer:** At least three.

- Why that solution fits the root cause.
- How it should be implemented.

**Expected answer:** The recommendation must logically address the identified root cause and show practical value for employees and organizational performance.

## Page 03 - Case Studies 1-5

### Original Page Content

### Case Studies (One per Group)

### Case Study 1 - High Employee Turnover

A mid-sized Sri Lankan IT company has recently experienced a significant increase in employee turnover.

Many experienced software engineers are leaving the organization within **1-2 years** of joining.

Exit interviews reveal that employees are attracted by:

- Higher salaries.
- Better work-life balance.
- Remote work opportunities offered by foreign companies.

As a result:

- Ongoing projects are frequently disrupted.
- Deadlines are missed.
- The company spends considerable time and money recruiting and training new employees.

### Case Study 2 - Poor Recruitment Decisions

A fast-growing IT company needed to quickly recruit developers for a new international project.

Due to time pressure, hiring decisions were based mainly on **academic qualifications rather than technical competence**.

Many recruits struggled to perform, leading to:

- Poor code quality.
- Project delays.
- Increased workload for experienced developers.

### Case Study 3 - Low Employee Motivation

Employees in a software development company complete their assigned work but rarely:

- Show initiative.
- Contribute innovative ideas.

The organization has:

- Little recognition for good performance.
- Employees who feel undervalued.

As a result:

- Productivity has steadily declined.
- Creativity has steadily declined.

### Case Study 4 - Ineffective Performance Management

An IT company conducts performance appraisals only **once a year** with minimal feedback throughout the year.

Employees are:

- Unclear about expectations.
- Unclear about career progression.

At the same time:

- High performers feel overlooked.
- Poor performers are not supported in improving their performance.

### Case Study 5 - Weak Reward System

**The case description continues on Page 04.**

### Supplementary Definitions

**Definition: Employee turnover**  
The rate or pattern at which employees leave an organization and are replaced.

**Simple meaning:** How often staff leave the company.

**Definition: Recruitment and selection**  
Processes used to attract candidates and choose people who fit job requirements.

**Simple meaning:** Finding applicants and selecting the right person for the job.

**Definition: Employee motivation**  
The forces that influence employees' effort, persistence, initiative, and willingness to perform.

**Simple meaning:** What makes employees want to contribute and perform well.

**Definition: Performance management**  
An ongoing process of setting expectations, monitoring performance, giving feedback, and supporting improvement.

**Simple meaning:** Continuously helping employees know what is expected and how to improve.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

These cases show that HR problems directly affect software projects.

```text
HR problem
   |
   v
Employee behavior / capability changes
   |
   v
Team performance changes
   |
   v
Project quality, cost, schedule, or innovation is affected
```

### Exam Tip

When analyzing Cases 1-4, always include both:

- **Employee-level impact**
- **Project/organization-level impact**

### Common Mistake

Do not treat turnover as only a recruitment problem. The source identifies salary, work-life balance, and remote work as reasons employees leave, so retention factors must also be analyzed.

### Likely Exam / Presentation Questions

**Question 1:** What factors are driving turnover in Case Study 1?  
**Expected answer:** Higher salaries, better work-life balance, and remote-work opportunities offered by foreign companies.

**Question 2:** What was wrong with recruitment in Case Study 2?  
**Expected answer:** Hiring decisions focused mainly on academic qualifications instead of technical competence.

### Original Page Content

### Case Study 5 - Weak Reward System (continued from Page 03)

A technology company provides similar rewards to both high-performing and average employees.

Employees perceive the reward system as unfair, with limited:

- Recognition.
- Career development opportunities.

An Agile software development team is led by a manager who:

- Poor collaboration.
- Low morale.
- Slower decision-making.

### Case Study 7 - Conflict in Project Teams

Developers and QA engineers frequently disagree about:

- Software quality.

Poor communication and lack of collaboration have increased workplace conflict, causing:

- Missed deadlines.

### Case Study 8 - Lack of Skills Development

An IT company has adopted:

- Cloud computing.
- AI technologies.

However, many employees lack the required technical skills.

Limited training opportunities have left employees feeling overwhelmed, resulting in:

- Lower productivity.
- Project delays.

### Case Study 9 - Poor Communication

Employees working on a software project are unclear about:

- Their roles.
- Their responsibilities.

Problems include:

- Inconsistent instructions from management.
- Weak communication between teams.
- Duplication of work.
- Missed deadlines.

### Case Analysis Aid

| Case | HRM Focus | Root Problem Stated in Source | Main Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Rewards and recognition | Similar rewards regardless of performance; limited recognition/development/incentives | Lower motivation and retention |
| 6 | Leadership | Manager dominates decisions and participation | Poor collaboration, low morale, slower decisions |

| 8 | Training and development | New technologies adopted without sufficient skills/training | Employees overwhelmed, productivity down, delays |
| 9 | Communication and role clarity | Inconsistent instructions and weak inter-team communication | Duplicated work and missed deadlines |

### Supplementary Definitions

**Definition: Reward system**  
The set of financial and non-financial benefits, recognition, and incentives used to acknowledge employee contribution.

**Simple meaning:** How the organization rewards employees.

**Definition: Leadership**  
The process of influencing and guiding people toward shared goals.

**Simple meaning:** How a leader helps a team decide, cooperate, and perform.

**Definition: Workplace conflict**  
Disagreement between individuals or groups that affects working relationships or performance.

**Simple meaning:** Work-related disagreement that becomes disruptive.

**Definition: Training and development**  
Activities that build employee skills, knowledge, and future capability.

**Simple meaning:** Helping employees learn what they need for current and future work.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

The common pattern on this page is that poor people-management systems reduce software-team effectiveness.

For example, the Agile-team case is not merely a meeting problem. Dominating decisions reduces participation, which weakens collaboration and can slow decisions rather than speed them up.

### Exam Tip

For every case, identify the **HR function involved**:

- Reward management
- Leadership / employee participation
- Conflict management
- Training and development
- Communication / role clarity

### Common Mistake

Do not recommend training for every HR problem. Case 8 clearly involves a skills gap, but Cases 5-7 and 9 require different interventions.

### Likely Exam / Presentation Questions

**Question 1:** What is the key leadership problem in Case Study 6?  
**Expected answer:** The manager makes decisions independently and discourages team participation, even in Agile stand-ups.

**Question 2:** How does poor communication affect Case Study 9?  
**Expected answer:** Employees become unclear about roles and responsibilities, causing duplicated work and missed deadlines.

## Page 05 - Case Studies 10-13

### Original Page Content

### Case Study 10 - Personality Clashes in Teams

A software project team consists of individuals with very different personalities and working styles.

Problems include:

- Introverted employees rarely contributing during meetings.
- More outspoken team members dominating discussions.

These differences have reduced:

- Collaboration.
- Overall team effectiveness.

### Case Study 11 - Employee Burnout Due to Continuous Project Deadlines

A software company has experienced rapid business growth, resulting in employees working overtime for several months to meet aggressive project deadlines.

Many employees report:

- Stress.
- Fatigue.
- Reduced work-life balance.
- Declining job satisfaction.

Additional effects:

- Absenteeism has increased.
- Several experienced employees have expressed intentions to resign.

### Case Study 12 - Managing a Hybrid Workforce

An IT organization has adopted a hybrid working model where:

- Some employees work remotely.
- Others work from the office.

Managers have noticed:

- Reduced collaboration.
- Inconsistent communication.
- Difficulties maintaining employee engagement.
- Difficulties maintaining team cohesion across different work locations.

### Case Study 13 - Diversity and Inclusion Challenges

An international software company employs people from different:

- Cultures.
- Nationalities.
- Generations.

Misunderstandings caused by:

- Communication styles.
- Cultural differences.
- Unconscious bias.

These issues have affected:

- Teamwork.
- Employee relationships.

The company wants to build a more inclusive workplace culture.

### Visual Explanation

The page uses four separate case-study blocks. The cases shift from conventional HR systems toward team dynamics, employee well-being, new work arrangements, and inclusion.

### Case Analysis Aid

| Case | Main HRM Theme | Employee / Team Problem | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Personality and team dynamics | Some voices are absent while others dominate | Reduced collaboration and team effectiveness |
| 11 | Employee well-being / workload | Stress, fatigue, poor work-life balance, low satisfaction | Absenteeism and resignation intention |
| 12 | Hybrid workforce management | Remote/office split weakens communication and cohesion | Lower collaboration and engagement |
| 13 | Diversity and inclusion | Cultural/style differences and unconscious bias | Damaged teamwork and relationships |

### Supplementary Definitions

**Definition: Burnout**  
A state of severe work-related exhaustion associated with prolonged stress and excessive demands.

**Simple meaning:** Employees become mentally and physically exhausted because work pressure continues too long.

**Definition: Hybrid workforce**  
A workforce in which employees perform work through a mix of remote and office-based arrangements.

**Simple meaning:** Some work happens from home or elsewhere, while some happens in the office.

**Definition: Diversity**  
The presence of people with different backgrounds, characteristics, experiences, and perspectives.

**Simple meaning:** A workforce made up of different kinds of people.

**Definition: Inclusion**  
Creating an environment where people are respected, able to participate, and treated as valued members.

**Simple meaning:** Making sure different employees can contribute and belong.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

These cases show that a technically skilled team can still underperform when:

- Meeting participation is unbalanced.
- Workloads are unsustainable.
- Remote and office employees become disconnected.
- Cultural differences and bias are poorly managed.

### Exam Tip

For Case 11, connect burnout to measurable organizational outcomes already stated in the case:

`Overtime -> stress/fatigue -> job dissatisfaction -> absenteeism + resignation intention`

### Common Mistake

Do not describe diversity as the problem in Case 13. The stated problems are misunderstandings, communication differences, cultural differences, and unconscious bias. Diversity itself is not identified as the failure.

### Likely Exam / Presentation Questions

**Question 1:** What indicators of burnout are visible in Case Study 11?  
**Expected answer:** Stress, fatigue, poor work-life balance, declining job satisfaction, absenteeism, and intention to resign.

**Question 2:** What challenges appear in the hybrid-work case?  
**Expected answer:** Reduced collaboration, inconsistent communication, lower engagement, and difficulty maintaining team cohesion.

### Supplementary Definitions

**Definition: Resistance to change**  
Employee reluctance or opposition to new organizational processes, structures, systems, or practices.

**Simple meaning:** Employees do not readily accept a change.

**Definition: Knowledge sharing**  
The process through which employees transfer useful experience, technical information, and practical know-how to others.

**Simple meaning:** Making sure important knowledge does not stay only in one person's head.

**Definition: Organizational ethics**  
Principles of fairness, integrity, consistency, and responsible conduct within organizational decisions and behavior.

**Simple meaning:** Making workplace decisions in a fair, transparent, and principled way.

### Student-Friendly Explanation

These cases emphasize long-term organizational capability.

- Change fails when employees fear negative consequences and are not ready to adopt it.
- Retention is not only about keeping people; organizations must also preserve their knowledge.
- HR decisions must be fair and consistent because perceived unfairness damages trust.

### Exam Tip

For Case 15, separate two issues:

1. **Employee retention**
2. **Knowledge retention**

Even if resignations cannot be fully prevented, documentation and formal knowledge sharing can reduce project risk.

### Common Mistake

Do not treat Case 16 as only an employee-relations problem. The source explicitly raises organizational ethics and transparency.

**Question 2:** What organizational weaknesses worsen the impact of senior resignations in Case Study 15?  
**Expected answer:** Limited documentation and the absence of a formal knowledge-sharing process.

# Full Workshop Summary

Workshop 2 applies Human Resource Management concepts to sixteen IT-industry scenarios. Students must prepare a 10-minute, approximately 6-8 slide group presentation. The presentation must introduce the organization and issue, identify problems, analyze root causes using HRM concepts and theories, identify relevant HR functions, propose at least three practical solutions, explain IT-industry relevance, and conclude with the main findings.

The marking scheme emphasizes analytical quality. Understanding and application of HRM concepts is worth 20%, analysis and critical thinking 25%, and practicality and justification of recommendations 25%. Presentation quality, collaboration, and Q&A make up the remaining 30%.

The case studies cover major HRM challenges:

- Employee turnover and retention.
- Recruitment and selection.
- Employee motivation.
- Performance management.
- Reward systems.
- Leadership.
- Project-team conflict.
- Training and skills development.
- Communication.
- Personality differences.
- Burnout and work-life balance.
- Hybrid workforce management.
- Diversity and inclusion.
- Resistance to change.
- Knowledge sharing and retention.
- Ethical HR decision-making.

A useful case-analysis workflow is:

```text
1. Read the case carefully.
2. Identify the specific HRM problem.
3. Separate symptoms from root causes.
4. Identify the employees and teams affected.
5. Explain the effect on project and organizational performance.
6. Identify the relevant HR functions.
7. Apply suitable HRM concepts/theories from the lecture.
8. Propose at least three practical solutions.
9. Justify each recommendation.
10. Explain how the solution improves:
    - Project performance
    - Employee satisfaction
    - Organizational effectiveness
```

# Likely Exam and Presentation Questions

## Question 1
What is the purpose of the workshop?

**Expected Answer:**  
To apply HRM concepts to real-world IT-industry scenarios, analyze organizational challenges, and propose practical, theory-based solutions.

## Question 2
What should be included in the analysis section of the presentation?

**Expected Answer:**  
Root causes should be analyzed using relevant HRM concepts and theories, and the HR functions involved should be identified.

## Question 3
Why is high employee turnover harmful to an IT company?

**Expected Answer:**  
It disrupts projects, causes missed deadlines, removes experienced staff, and increases recruitment and training costs.

## Question 4
What is wrong with the recruitment process in Case Study 2?

**Expected Answer:**  
Candidates were selected mainly on academic qualifications rather than technical competence.

## Question 5
Why does the weak reward system reduce motivation?

**Expected Answer:**  
High and average performers receive similar rewards, while recognition, career development, and performance-based incentives are limited, creating perceived unfairness.

## Question 6
Why is the leadership style in Case Study 6 unsuitable for an Agile team?

**Expected Answer:**  
The manager dominates decisions and discourages participation, reducing collaboration, morale, and decision-making effectiveness.

## Question 7
What HRM issue is central to Case Study 8?

**Expected Answer:**  
A skills gap combined with insufficient training and development after the adoption of cloud and AI technologies.

## Question 8
What are the visible consequences of burnout in Case Study 11?

**Expected Answer:**  
Stress, fatigue, reduced work-life balance, declining job satisfaction, increased absenteeism, and intention to resign.

## Question 9
What makes hybrid workforce management difficult in the case?

**Expected Answer:**  
Reduced collaboration, inconsistent communication, weaker engagement, and difficulty maintaining team cohesion across locations.

## Question 10
Why is knowledge sharing important in Case Study 15?

**Expected Answer:**  
Senior employees are leaving with technical knowledge, while limited documentation and no formal sharing process make new employees slow to become productive and delay project delivery.

## Question 11
What ethical problems are identified in Case Study 16?

**Expected Answer:**  
Favoritism in promotions, inconsistent disciplinary actions, and unequal access to training.

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Describing the case without identifying the HRM problem.
- Treating symptoms as root causes.
- Recommending solutions before analyzing the cause.
- Using generic HR theories without connecting them to the case.
- Ignoring the effect on IT project performance.
- Giving fewer than three practical recommendations.
- Failing to justify recommendations.
- Treating turnover only as a recruitment issue.
- Assuming academic qualifications automatically prove technical competence.
- Treating annual appraisal alone as effective performance management.
- Treating Agile leadership as command-and-control decision-making.
- Assuming every team conflict requires the same solution.
- Treating diversity itself as a problem instead of focusing on bias, communication, and inclusion issues.
- Treating employee retention and knowledge retention as identical.
- Ignoring fairness and transparency when discussing HR ethics.
