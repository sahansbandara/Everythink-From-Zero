## Page 08 - Marketing Strategy — From Vision to Execution

### Original Page Content

**Marketing Strategy**

An effective marketing strategy maps, high-level goals down to tactical activities. It ensures that every marketing campaign, budget allocation, and asset created directly supports the long-term vision of the company. E.g.

- Vision: "Become the safest cloud-storage platform for global finance firms"

- Strategy: "Position our brand around military- grade encryption and target mid-sized banks"

- Target Market: "Chief Information Security Officers at fintech companies with 50-500 employees"

- Execution: "Publish weekly whitepapers, run highly targeted LinkedIn ads, and attend regional banking cyber security expos"

### Recreated Strategy Hierarchy

```text
VISION
The big "Why" - long-term destination and product identity
        |
        v
STRATEGY
The roadmap - core positioning, goals, and value definition
        |
        v
TARGET MARKET
The "Who" - audience segmentation and persona profiling
        |
        v
EXECUTION
The "How" - daily tactics, content creation, and ad spend
```

### Source Example

| Level | Example from slide |
|---|---|
| Vision | "Become the safest cloud-storage platform for global finance firms" |
| Strategy | "Position our brand around military-grade encryption and target mid-sized banks" |
| Target Market | "Chief Information Security Officers at fintech companies with 50-500 employees" |
| Execution | "Publish weekly whitepapers, run highly targeted LinkedIn ads, and attend regional banking cyber security expos" |

### Visual Explanation

A descending four-level diagram maps **Vision → Strategy → Target Market → Execution**, showing how a high-level company direction becomes daily tactics.

### Explanation (Supplementary)

Good strategy connects abstract goals to a specific target market and concrete execution. Each lower level should directly support the level above it.

### Exam Tip

Use the hierarchy: Vision → Strategy → Target Market → Execution.

### Common Mistake

Do not jump straight from Vision to tactics without defining strategy and target market.

## Page 11 - STP Model

### Original Page Content

**STP Model**

- The STP Model (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) is a key marketing strategy framework.

- It helps businesses divide a broad market into smaller groups, select the most valuable groups to focus on, and create a clear, appealing message in the minds of those specific customers Segmentation: Dividing the market into smaller groups based on demographics, behavior and preferences. (Age / Gender/ Location) Targeting: Selecting the most attractive customer segments based on profitability, needs and growth potential, Size, Accessibility Positioning: Creating a unique image of the product in the customer’s mind by focusing  on value, experience and differentiation.

### Recreated STP Table

| Stage | Meaning | Slide factors/examples |
|---|---|---|
| Segmentation | Divide the market into smaller groups | Demographics, behavior, preferences; age, gender, location |
| Targeting | Select the most attractive customer segments | Profitability, needs, growth potential, size, accessibility |
| Positioning | Create a unique image in the customer's mind | Value, experience, differentiation |

### Visual Explanation

Three illustrated columns represent **Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning**, visually reinforcing the STP sequence from grouping customers to choosing groups to shaping perception.

### Explanation (Supplementary)

STP narrows a broad market into useful customer groups, selects which groups deserve focus, then creates a distinct position for the chosen group.

### Definition

**Definition: Segmentation** - Dividing a broad market into smaller groups based on demographics, behavior and preferences.

**Simple meaning:** Split the market into groups.

**Definition: Targeting** - Selecting the most attractive customer segments based on profitability, needs, growth potential, size and accessibility.

**Simple meaning:** Choose which groups to focus on.

**Definition: Positioning** - Creating a unique image of the product in the customer’s mind by focusing on value, experience and differentiation.

**Simple meaning:** Decide how the chosen customer should perceive the product.

### Exam Tip

Memorize STP in order: Segmentation → Targeting → Positioning.

### Common Mistake

Segmentation divides; Targeting selects; Positioning shapes perception.

## Page 17 - Product Management — Customer, Technology and Business Fit

**Product Management**

- Process of guiding a product from an initial idea to its final release and beyond.

  - Can we build this? (Technology)

- Decisions are guided by customer data, feedback, and usage patterns to continuously improve product performance and relevance.

- Advanced technologies and innovative features are incorporated to enhance product functionality, usability, and user experience.

## Page 18 - Product Life Cycle

### Original Page Content

**Product Life Cycle**

Stages of the Product Life Cycle

1. Introduction: Product is launched and awareness is created.

2. Growth: Sales increase and the product gains popularity.

3. Maturity: Market becomes saturated and competition is high.

4. Decline: Sales decrease as demand reduces or new alternatives emerge.

The Product Life Cycle (PLC) describes the stages a product goes through from its introduction to its decline in the market.


| Stage | Source meaning |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Product is launched and awareness is created |
| Growth | Sales increase and the product gains popularity |
| Maturity | Market becomes saturated and competition is high |
| Decline | Sales decrease as demand reduces or new alternatives emerge |

Two Product Life Cycle charts show Sales over Time through **Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline**. A second curve illustrates extending/restarting growth through actions such as releasing a new version or selling in a different country.

### Explanation (Supplementary)

The PLC explains how market conditions change over time, which means product and marketing actions should change as the product moves from introduction to decline.

### Definition

**Definition: Product Life Cycle (PLC)** - The stages a product goes through from introduction to decline in the market.

### Exam Tip

PLC order: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline.

### Common Mistake

# Full Lecture Summary

Marketing in this lecture is defined as a **human-centric and technology-enabled** process for creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging customer value while building meaningful long-term relationships. Marketing differs from selling because marketing is customer-focused, long-term and relationship-oriented, while selling is product-focused, short-term and transaction-oriented. The lecture traces marketing from product-centric production efficiency to customer satisfaction, values/emotion, digital engagement, data/AI personalization and the Marketing 6.0 human-plus-technology experience era.

Marketing strategy provides long-term direction and maps **Vision -> Strategy -> Target Market -> Execution**. The strategy is executed through the **Marketing Mix (Product, Price, Place, Promotion)**. The **STP model** divides the market into segments, selects attractive target segments and positions the product in the chosen customer’s mind. A **Customer Value Proposition (CVP)** explains the unique value that makes the customer choose one offering over competitors.

Market research gathers, analyzes and interprets customer/market/competitor information. It uses primary data such as surveys/interviews and secondary data such as reports/databases. Digital tools provide real-time, large-scale behavioural data; AI speeds pattern discovery, predictive analytics, forecasting, personalization and targeted marketing.

Product management guides a product from idea through release and beyond. It balances **customer need**, **technology feasibility**, and **business strategy**, and uses customer data, feedback, Agile and MVP approaches for continuous improvement. The Product Life Cycle progresses through **Introduction, Growth, Maturity and Decline**. IT products are usually continuously updated digital solutions such as SaaS, mobile applications and platforms, with strong emphasis on usability, analytics, personalization and integration.

Marketing 6.0 focuses on immersive **phygital** experiences. IT enables it through data/analytics, automation, personalization, platform integration, CRM, AR/VR/metaverse experiences, real-time decision making and enhanced customer experience. Digital marketing uses internet/electronic channels such as websites, search engines, social media, email and paid ads, and evaluates results using quantifiable metrics such as CTR, Conversion Rate and Customer Acquisition Cost.

Brand management builds and maintains brand identity, image and customer-perceived value using consistent communication, emotional connections, digital storytelling and positive experiences. **Brand equity** is the added value created by awareness, trust, loyalty, perceptions and experiences. Experience-driven branding emphasizes meaningful, memorable experiences at every touchpoint.

CRM manages and strengthens customer interactions for long-term relationships and loyalty. Its objectives are customer retention, customer loyalty, Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), and personalized customer experience. CRM tools centralize customer data, communication, automation and analytics. The lecture examples are Salesforce, HubSpot and Zoho CRM. Finally, **Customer Experience (CX)** is the overall perception and feelings created by every interaction with the brand across all touchpoints; strong CX is seamless, consistent, functional, emotional, digital, personalized and technology-enabled.

# Likely Exam Questions

1. **Define marketing according to the lecture.**  
   **Expected answer:** A human-centric and technology-enabled process of creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging value while building meaningful long-term customer relationships.

2. **Differentiate marketing and selling.**  
   **Expected answer:** Marketing is customer-focused, long-term and relationship-oriented; selling is product-focused, short-term and transaction-oriented.

3. **Explain the 4Ps of the marketing mix.**  
   **Expected answer:** Product = offering/value; Price = pricing relative to demand/competitors; Place = distribution; Promotion = message/channels.

4. **Explain the STP model.**  
   **Expected answer:** Segment the market, target attractive segments, position the product distinctly for those customers.

5. **What is a Customer Value Proposition?**  
   **Expected answer:** The unique value explaining why customers should choose the product/service over competitors.

6. **Why is market research important?**  
   **Expected answer:** It reveals needs/preferences/behaviour, supports informed decisions, reduces uncertainty and improves strategy effectiveness.

7. **What three questions guide product management in Page 17?**  
   **Expected answer:** Does the customer need this? Can we build this? Does this make sense for the business?

8. **List the stages of the Product Life Cycle.**  
   **Expected answer:** Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline.

9. **What is Marketing 6.0?**  
   **Expected answer:** The next marketing stage focused on immersive phygital experiences by combining human understanding with advanced digital tools.

10. **Name four IT enablers of Marketing 6.0.**  
    **Expected answer:** Any four source items, e.g. analytics, automation, personalization, integration, CRM, immersive technologies, real-time decisions, enhanced CX.

11. **Define brand equity.**  
    **Expected answer:** The value a brand adds based on customer perceptions, trust and experiences, strengthened by awareness, loyalty and consistent branding.

12. **State four CRM objectives.**  
    **Expected answer:** Customer Retention, Customer Loyalty, Customer Lifetime Value, Personalized Customer Experience.

13. **Compare the CRM examples in the lecture.**  
    **Expected answer:** Salesforce = large-scale/data-driven/AI; HubSpot = SME-friendly/all-in-one/inbound; Zoho = cost-effective/customizable/startup/SME.

14. **Define Customer Experience (CX).**  
    **Expected answer:** The overall perception and feelings a customer has from all interactions with a brand across different touchpoints.
