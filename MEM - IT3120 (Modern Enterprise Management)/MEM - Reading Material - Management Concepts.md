## Page 01 — Case Study: NexTech Solutions (Pvt) Ltd — Growing through People and Innovation

### Original Slide Content

### Background

NexTech Solutions (Pvt) Ltd is a medium-sized Sri Lankan software company established in 2015 by three graduates from a local university. Starting with only six employees developing custom web applications for local businesses, the company has grown rapidly and now employs over 320 professionals, including:

- Software engineers
- Quality assurance engineers
- UX designers
- DevOps engineers
- Data scientists
- Business analysts
- Project managers
- Support staff

Today, NexTech serves clients in:

- Sri Lanka
- Australia
- Singapore
- The United Kingdom
- The Middle East

The company specializes in:

- Artificial Intelligence (AI)
- Cloud computing
- Enterprise software solutions
- Fintech applications
- Mobile application development

As the company expanded internationally, the founders realised that technical expertise alone was no longer sufficient. Managing people, maintaining quality, encouraging innovation, and sustaining employee motivation became equally important for long-term success.

### Organizational Structure

Initially, NexTech operated with a **flat organizational structure**, where every employee reported directly to one of the founders. Decision-making was quick, communication was informal, and employees enjoyed considerable autonomy.

However, as the company expanded, managing hundreds of employees through a flat structure became increasingly difficult. The founders introduced a **functional organizational structure** with specialized departments including:

- Software Engineering
- Artificial Intelligence & Data Science
- Quality Assurance
- Project Management
- Human Resources
- Finance
- Marketing & Sales
- Research & Innovation

Each department is led by a Department Manager who reports to the Chief Executive Officer (CEO). Cross-functional project teams are formed for each client project, bringing together employees from multiple departments.

### Explanation

This page shows how organizational structure must change when a company grows. A flat structure was suitable when NexTech had only a few employees because direct communication with the founders was manageable. Once the workforce exceeded 320 people, the same structure created a management bottleneck.

The functional structure solves this problem by grouping people according to expertise. Department Managers provide clear supervision and responsibility. However, client software projects require multiple skills, so NexTech also forms cross-functional teams.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Management**  
The process of planning, organising, leading, and controlling organizational resources to achieve goals effectively and efficiently.

**Simple meaning:** Management means deciding what must be done, arranging people and resources, guiding employees, and checking whether results meet the plan.

**Definition: Flat organizational structure**  
An organizational structure with few or no middle-management levels between employees and senior leaders.

**Simple meaning:** Employees report directly to top leaders, so communication is fast, but the structure becomes difficult to manage as the company grows.

**Definition: Functional organizational structure**  
An organizational structure that groups employees according to specialized functions such as Engineering, Finance, HR, or Marketing.

**Simple meaning:** People who perform similar work belong to the same department.

**Definition: Cross-functional team**  
A team made up of employees from different departments who work together toward one project or objective.

**Simple meaning:** A software project may combine engineers, QA staff, UX designers, project managers, and data scientists.

### Comparison: Flat vs Functional Structure

| Factor | Flat Structure | Functional Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Management levels | Few | Several specialized levels |
| Communication | Direct and informal | More formal and departmental |
| Decision speed | Usually fast | May be slower due to hierarchy |
| Employee autonomy | High | Depends on departmental rules |
| Best suited for | Small/start-up organizations | Medium and large organizations |
| Main risk | Managers become overloaded as the firm grows | Departmental silos may develop |
| NexTech example | Six-employee startup phase | 320+ employee expansion phase |

### Exam Tip

For a case-study question, identify both the **original flat structure** and the **later functional structure**. Explain *why* the change occurred: rapid growth made direct founder supervision impractical.

### Common Mistake

Do not say the company completely abandoned teamwork after introducing departments. It uses a **functional structure for permanent departments** and **cross-functional teams for client projects**.

### Likely Exam Questions

#### Question 1
Why did NexTech change from a flat structure to a functional structure?

**Expected answer:**  
The flat structure became difficult to manage as the company grew beyond 320 employees. A functional structure introduced specialized departments, Department Managers, clearer responsibilities, and a more manageable reporting system.

#### Question 2
What is a cross-functional team? Give an example from the case.

**Expected answer:**  
A cross-functional team contains members from different departments. NexTech forms client project teams using employees from Engineering, QA, UX, Project Management, Data Science, and DevOps.

## Page 02 — Management Style, Motivation, and Organizational Culture

### Original Slide Content

### Management Style

The CEO believes that software professionals perform best when they are trusted and empowered rather than closely supervised.

Instead of giving detailed instructions, managers encourage employees to:

- Participate in decision-making
- Contribute ideas during sprint planning meetings
- Propose innovative solutions to client problems

Team leaders conduct weekly one-on-one meetings, focusing on coaching rather than monitoring.

However, during critical client deliveries or cybersecurity incidents, managers temporarily adopt a more directive leadership approach to ensure deadlines are met and risks are minimized.

### Motivation and Employee Engagement

Competition for talented software engineers in Sri Lanka is intense. To retain skilled employees, NexTech has introduced:

- Flexible working hours
- Hybrid working arrangements
- Performance bonuses
- Annual salary reviews
- Professional certification sponsorship
- Conference participation
- Innovation awards
- Internal hackathons
- Wellness programmes
- Career mentoring

Employees are encouraged to spend one day each month working on personal innovation projects. Several successful AI products developed during these innovation days have later become commercial solutions.

The company also recognises employees publicly during monthly town hall meetings, celebrating:

- Technical achievements
- Customer appreciation
- Teamwork

### Leadership and Organizational Culture

The founders believe that innovation depends on collaboration rather than hierarchy.

Managers maintain an open-door policy, encouraging employees to discuss:

- New ideas
- Workplace concerns
- Technical challenges

Employees can raise these matters without fear of criticism.

### Visual Explanation

The page shows that NexTech does not use one management style in every situation. Its normal style is empowering and participative, but it becomes directive during urgent or high-risk situations.

#### Employee Motivation System

| Motivation category | NexTech initiatives | Intended effect |
|---|---|---|
| Work flexibility | Flexible hours, hybrid work | Work-life balance and retention |
| Financial rewards | Bonuses, salary reviews | Reward performance and remain competitive |
| Learning and development | Certifications, conferences, mentoring | Professional growth |
| Recognition | Innovation awards, town hall recognition | Appreciation and achievement |
| Innovation opportunities | Hackathons, monthly innovation day | Creativity and product development |
| Employee wellbeing | Wellness programmes | Health, satisfaction, and engagement |

### Explanation

NexTech applies a participative and coaching-oriented approach because knowledge workers often perform better when they can make decisions and contribute ideas. The company also recognises that one leadership style is not appropriate in every situation. During emergencies, a directive style can provide the clarity and speed required to protect the company and its clients.

The motivation system combines financial and non-financial rewards. Salary and bonuses are important, but professional development, recognition, flexibility, innovation opportunities, and wellbeing also affect retention.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Employee empowerment**  
Giving employees authority, confidence, information, and freedom to make decisions related to their work.

**Simple meaning:** Employees are trusted to solve problems instead of waiting for every instruction from a manager.

**Definition: Participative management**  
A management approach in which employees are involved in decisions that affect their work.

**Simple meaning:** Managers ask employees for ideas and include them in decision-making.

**Definition: Coaching leadership**  
A leadership style focused on developing employee skills through guidance, feedback, and support.

**Simple meaning:** The leader helps employees improve rather than only checking or controlling them.

**Definition: Directive leadership**  
A leadership style in which the leader gives clear instructions and closely coordinates work.

**Simple meaning:** The manager tells employees exactly what must be done, especially during urgent situations.

**Definition: Situational leadership**  
The practice of changing leadership style according to the needs of the situation, task, and employees.

**Simple meaning:** A manager may be participative during normal work and directive during a crisis.

**Definition: Employee engagement**  
The level of emotional commitment, involvement, and enthusiasm employees have toward their work and organization.

**Simple meaning:** Engaged employees care about the company and willingly contribute more effort and ideas.

**Definition: Organizational culture**  
The shared values, beliefs, behaviours, and working practices that influence how people act inside an organization.

**Simple meaning:** Culture is “how things are normally done” in the company.

### Exam Tip

When asked to identify NexTech's leadership style, do not give only one label. The case demonstrates **participative**, **coaching**, and **situational leadership**, with a **directive approach during emergencies**.

### Common Mistake

Do not treat motivation as salary only. NexTech uses both:

- **Financial motivation:** bonuses and salary reviews
- **Non-financial motivation:** recognition, flexibility, learning, mentoring, wellness, and innovation opportunities

### Likely Exam Questions

#### Question 1
How does NexTech demonstrate situational leadership?

**Expected answer:**  
Managers normally use participative and coaching leadership, but temporarily use a directive approach during critical client deliveries or cybersecurity incidents.

#### Question 2
Identify four non-financial methods used by NexTech to motivate employees.

**Expected answer:**  
Any four: flexible working, hybrid work, certification sponsorship, conference participation, innovation awards, hackathons, wellness programmes, career mentoring, or public recognition.

## Page 03 — Continuous Learning, Power, Decision-Making, and Communication

### Original Slide Content

### Continuous Learning

The company promotes a culture of continuous learning through:

- Technical communities of practice
- Internal knowledge-sharing sessions
- Research seminars
- Mentoring programmes
- Graduate training schemes

Employees from different departments regularly collaborate on innovation projects and research publications.

### Power and Decision-Making

While the CEO has ultimate authority over strategic decisions, operational decisions are largely decentralized.

- Project Managers decide project schedules.
- Technical Architects decide system architecture.
- HR Managers oversee recruitment and employee development.
- Team Leaders allocate daily tasks.

Experienced software engineers often influence technical decisions because of their expertise, even when they do not hold formal managerial positions.

The company recognises that expert knowledge can be just as influential as formal authority.

### Communication

NexTech uses multiple communication channels:

- Microsoft Teams
- Jira
- Confluence
- Email
- Weekly sprint reviews
- Monthly town hall meetings
- Quarterly strategic planning sessions

Senior management shares company performance, future strategies, financial updates, and client success stories during quarterly meetings to promote transparency.

| Delegated authority | Project Managers, Technical Architects, HR Managers, Team Leaders | Responsibility is assigned for specific areas |

### Explanation

NexTech decentralizes many operational decisions so that decisions are made by people with the most relevant knowledge. The CEO still controls major strategic decisions, but managers and specialists handle decisions within their areas.

The case also distinguishes **authority** from **power**. A senior engineer may not have a managerial title, but technical expertise can strongly influence decisions. This is expert power.

The communication system combines digital platforms and scheduled meetings. Different channels serve different purposes, from daily task coordination to strategic transparency.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Continuous learning**  
An organizational commitment to regularly developing knowledge, skills, and capabilities.

**Simple meaning:** Employees keep learning instead of depending only on what they already know.

**Definition: Power**  
The ability to influence the behaviour, decisions, or actions of other people.

**Simple meaning:** Power means being able to affect what others do.

**Definition: Authority**  
The formal right attached to a position to make decisions, issue instructions, and allocate resources.

**Simple meaning:** Authority comes from an official job role.

**Definition: Legitimate power**  
Influence that comes from a person's formal organizational position.

**Simple meaning:** Employees follow the CEO because the CEO officially leads the company.

**Definition: Expert power**  
Influence that comes from specialized knowledge, skills, or experience.

**Simple meaning:** People listen to senior engineers because they understand the technology deeply.

**Definition: Decentralized decision-making**  
Distributing decision authority to lower levels or specialized employees instead of keeping all decisions at the top.

**Simple meaning:** The CEO does not make every operational decision.

**Definition: Organizational communication**  
The formal and informal exchange of information among employees, teams, managers, and stakeholders.

**Simple meaning:** It is how information moves through the company.

### Exam Tip

A strong answer should explain that NexTech uses **centralized strategic decisions** and **decentralized operational decisions**. Use specific examples from the case.

### Common Mistake

Do not confuse power and authority:

- **Authority** is the formal right to decide.
- **Power** is the ability to influence.
- A senior engineer can have expert power without formal authority.

### Likely Exam Questions

#### Question 1
Explain how decision-making is decentralized at NexTech.

**Expected answer:**  
Operational decisions are delegated to specialized roles: Project Managers set schedules, Technical Architects select system architecture, HR Managers manage recruitment and development, and Team Leaders allocate daily tasks.

#### Question 2
Why do experienced software engineers have power even without managerial positions?

**Expected answer:**  
They possess expert power. Their specialized technical knowledge allows them to influence technical decisions even without formal authority.

## Page 04 — Managing Change, Ethical Management, and Looking Ahead

### Original Slide Content

### Managing Change

NexTech decided to integrate Generative AI into all software development teams.

Many younger employees welcomed the change, while some experienced developers expressed concerns about:

- Job security
- Reliability of AI-generated code

To support the transition, management introduced:

- AI training workshops
- Pilot projects
- Internal AI champions
- Peer mentoring
- Clear policies on responsible AI usage

Employees were invited to provide feedback throughout the implementation process, reducing resistance and increasing acceptance.

### Ethical Management

As the company increasingly develops AI-powered healthcare and financial applications, ethical considerations have become an important management responsibility.

A Technology Ethics Committee reviews AI solutions before deployment, ensuring compliance with:

- Privacy regulations
- Fairness principles
- Cybersecurity requirements
- Responsible AI guidelines

Employees receive annual training on:

- Professional ethics
- Information security
- Diversity
- Inclusion
- Responsible technology development

### Looking Ahead

NexTech plans to expand into Europe within the next three years.

Although the company has built a strong technical reputation, its leadership recognises that future success will depend on:

- Technological excellence
- Effective management
- Employee wellbeing
- Innovation
- Collaboration
- Ethical leadership

The founders often remind employees:

> “Great technology is created by great people working together.”

```text

      v       v        v            v

      |       |        |            |
```

### Explanation

Change often creates uncertainty. NexTech does not force AI adoption without support. It uses training, experiments, internal champions, mentoring, policies, and employee feedback. These actions address both skill gaps and emotional resistance.

Ethical management is especially important because mistakes in healthcare and finance can harm users. NexTech therefore creates a formal review mechanism before deployment and trains employees in responsible conduct.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Change management**  
A structured process for planning, implementing, and supporting organizational change while reducing resistance.

**Simple meaning:** It is how management helps people move from the current way of working to a new way.

**Definition: Resistance to change**  
Employee opposition, concern, hesitation, or unwillingness toward a proposed organizational change.

**Simple meaning:** Employees may resist because they fear uncertainty, job loss, or failure.

**Definition: Change champion**  
A person who actively supports a change and helps others understand and adopt it.

**Simple meaning:** An internal AI champion encourages and guides colleagues during AI adoption.

**Definition: Ethical management**  
Making and implementing organizational decisions according to moral principles, professional standards, legal requirements, and stakeholder wellbeing.

**Simple meaning:** Managers consider whether a decision is responsible and fair, not only whether it is profitable.

**Definition: Responsible AI**  
The design, development, and use of AI in a way that supports fairness, privacy, transparency, security, accountability, and human wellbeing.

**Simple meaning:** AI should be safe, fair, explainable, and used without harming people.

### Exam Tip

For a change-management answer, connect each action to its purpose:

- Training reduces skill gaps.
- Pilot projects reduce uncertainty.
- Champions and mentoring provide support.
- Policies clarify acceptable use.
- Feedback increases participation and acceptance.

### Common Mistake

Do not state that employee resistance is always negative or irrational. Concerns about job security and unreliable AI-generated code are legitimate risks that management must address.

### Likely Exam Questions

#### Question 1
How did NexTech reduce resistance to Generative AI adoption?

**Expected answer:**  
It provided AI training, pilot projects, internal AI champions, peer mentoring, responsible AI policies, and opportunities for employee feedback.

#### Question 2
Why is ethical management important for NexTech's AI solutions?

**Expected answer:**  
The company develops healthcare and financial systems where unfairness, privacy breaches, insecure systems, or unreliable AI could seriously harm users. Ethical review protects stakeholders and ensures compliance.

#### Case Mapping Table

### Explanation

This page converts the narrative case into recognized management concepts. The strongest framework is **Planning, Organising, Leading, and Controlling (POLC)**.

The company also changes its organizational structure as it grows and uses several leadership styles. Visionary leadership is visible in the company's international and technological direction. Transformational leadership appears through innovation, employee development, and organizational change.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Planning**  
Setting objectives and deciding in advance what actions are required to achieve them.

**Simple meaning:** Decide where the organization wants to go and how it will get there.

**Definition: Organising**  
Arranging tasks, people, authority, and resources so that plans can be implemented.

**Simple meaning:** Put the right people and resources into a workable structure.

**Definition: Leading**  
Influencing, guiding, motivating, and communicating with employees to achieve objectives.

**Simple meaning:** Help people work effectively toward the goal.

**Definition: Controlling**  
Monitoring performance, comparing results with plans or standards, and correcting deviations.

**Simple meaning:** Check whether work is going according to plan and fix problems.

**Definition: Visionary leadership**  
Leadership that creates and communicates a clear picture of the organization's desired future.

**Simple meaning:** Leaders show employees where the company is going.

**Definition: Transformational leadership**  
Leadership that inspires people to improve, innovate, and support significant organizational change.

**Simple meaning:** Leaders encourage employees and the organization to become better than before.

### Exam Tip

When answering a POLC question, include both the definition and one direct NexTech example for each function.

### Common Mistake

Do not describe controlling as punishing employees. In management, controlling means measuring performance, detecting deviations, and taking corrective action.

### Likely Exam Questions

#### Question 1
Apply the four functions of management to NexTech Solutions.

**Expected answer:**  
Planning includes international growth and AI adoption; organising includes functional departments and cross-functional teams; leading includes coaching and empowerment; controlling includes sprint reviews, performance evaluations, project monitoring, and ethical reviews.

#### Question 2
What leadership styles can be identified in the case?

**Expected answer:**  
Participative, democratic, coaching, situational, visionary, transformational, and temporary directive leadership during critical situations.

### Key Definitions

**Definition: Referent power**  
Influence based on admiration, respect, trust, or identification with a person.

**Simple meaning:** People follow a respected founder because they believe in the person and the vision.

**Definition: Reward power**  
Influence based on the ability to provide valued benefits or recognition.

# Full Lecture Summary

NexTech Solutions began as a six-person Sri Lankan software startup and developed into a medium-sized international software company with over 320 professionals. Its growth demonstrates that technical capability alone is not enough; organizations also require effective management of people, structure, motivation, quality, innovation, change, communication, and ethics.

The company initially used a flat organizational structure. This allowed fast decisions, informal communication, and employee autonomy. As the business expanded, the founders introduced a functional structure with specialized departments and Department Managers. Because software projects require multiple areas of expertise, NexTech also forms cross-functional project teams.

NexTech's normal management style is participative and coaching-oriented. Employees are empowered to contribute ideas and take part in decisions. During urgent client deliveries or cybersecurity incidents, managers temporarily use directive leadership. This demonstrates situational leadership.

The company motivates employees through a mixture of financial and non-financial practices: bonuses, salary reviews, flexible and hybrid work, recognition, certifications, conferences, mentoring, wellness programmes, innovation awards, hackathons, and monthly innovation days. These practices support employee engagement, retention, learning, and creativity.

Its organizational culture emphasizes innovation, collaboration, continuous learning, transparency, open communication, and ethical behaviour. Knowledge is developed through communities of practice, seminars, mentoring, internal sharing, and research collaboration.

Decision-making is partly centralized and partly decentralized. Executive management makes strategic decisions, while Project Managers, Technical Architects, HR Managers, Team Leaders, and experienced engineers make operational and specialist decisions. The case distinguishes formal authority from power, especially expert power.

NexTech uses multiple communication channels for different purposes, including Teams, Jira, Confluence, email, sprint reviews, town halls, and strategic planning sessions. Senior management communicates performance and strategy to promote transparency.

The company's Generative AI adoption demonstrates change management. Management addresses employee concerns using training, pilot projects, AI champions, peer mentoring, responsible AI policies, and feedback. These activities reduce resistance and increase acceptance.

Ethical management is formalized through a Technology Ethics Committee that reviews AI healthcare and financial solutions. Reviews focus on privacy, fairness, cybersecurity, and responsible AI. Employees receive ethics, security, diversity, and inclusion training.

The case demonstrates the four management functions: Planning, Organising, Leading, and Controlling. It also demonstrates organizational structure, leadership styles, power, authority, motivation, culture, communication, teamwork, decision-making, change management, HRM, innovation management, ethics, technology management, and CSR.

Future growth into Europe depends on combining technological excellence with people management, employee wellbeing, innovation, collaboration, ethical leadership, and social responsibility.

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Saying a flat structure is always better because decisions are faster.
- Saying a functional structure means departments never collaborate.
- Identifying only one leadership style when the case demonstrates several.
- Treating directive leadership as NexTech's normal management style.
- Describing motivation as salary and bonuses only.
- Confusing employee engagement with simple job attendance.
- Confusing authority with power.
- Calling expert power a formal position.
- Saying all NexTech decisions are decentralized.
- Ignoring the CEO's strategic authority.
- Describing controlling as punishment.
- Treating change management as simply announcing a new technology.
- Ignoring employee concerns about job security and AI reliability.
- Treating ethics as optional when systems affect healthcare and finance.
- Defining CSR only as charity or donations.
- Listing case facts without connecting them to management concepts.
