#### Module Description — Aim

This unit provides a broad introduction to the application of management principles in IT projects and operations. The unit introduces the management principles and skills needed for IT professionals to work in or manage IT projects and explore how organizations operate and the management within an organization. This unit also covers the key tools of project management, including planning, estimating, scheduling, budgeting, and resource allocation, team management. In addition, this unit also introduces the management and legal aspects related to entrepreneurship. This unit, in line with current industry practices, supports increased confidence and serves as a bridge between technical students and the real business world.

#### Learning Outcomes

At the end of the module, the student will be able to:

| LO | Learning outcome |
|---|---|
| LO1 | Explain the key organizational functions within IT businesses or Units |
| LO2 | Apply basic concepts of management to successfully manage IT organizations |
| LO3 | Explain the process and requirements of developing a new IT business organization |
| LO4 | Apply principles and tools of project management to the industrial environment |
| LO5 | Evaluate legal and ethical aspects of IT businesses and projects |

# Full Module Summary

IT3120 Modern Enterprise Management is a Year 3, Semester 1 module worth four credits. It introduces management principles for IT organizations, projects, and operations. The module combines organizational management, marketing, economics, HRM, finance, entrepreneurship, legal and ethical issues, and project management.

The first five learning areas primarily support LO1 and LO2 by developing knowledge of organizational functions and basic management application. Entrepreneurship supports LO3. Legal and ethical issues support LO5. The Project Management block supports LO4 and covers lifecycle/process groups and the management of stakeholders, communication, cost, scope, procurement, resources, schedule, risk, quality, and integration.

Assessment consists of an Assignment (30%), Midterm (20%), and Final Exam (50%). The Assignment targets LO4–LO5, the Midterm targets LO1–LO2, and the Final Exam covers LO1–LO5. The estimated workload is 200 hours, including 45 hours of lectures/tutorials, 30 workshop hours, 35 assessment hours, and 90 hours of independent study.

The module requires students to obtain 50% in both continuous assessment and the end-semester examination, resulting in an overall mark sufficient for a C grade or above. It also emphasizes academic honesty: discussion and reference-finding may be collaborative, but presenting another person’s work as one’s own is plagiarism.
