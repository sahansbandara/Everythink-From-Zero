# Tutorial Answer Guide

## 1. What is management?

**How to approach:** Start with a clear definition, name the four management functions, and state the purpose of management.

**Concept explanation:** Henri Fayol describes management as the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling the efforts of organizational members and using other organizational resources to achieve stated goals. Mary Parker Follett describes it as the art of getting things done through people.

**Definition:** Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling people and resources to achieve organizational goals.

**Simple meaning:** A manager decides what should be achieved, arranges the work, guides people, and checks the results.

> **Final answer:** Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling people and organizational resources to achieve stated goals effectively. It also involves getting work done through people by coordinating their efforts.

## 2. What is the difference between efficiency and effectiveness?

**How to approach:** Define both terms, then show that one concerns goal achievement while the other concerns resource use.

**Concept explanation:**

| Concept | Meaning | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Achieving the intended objective | “Did we achieve the correct goal?” |
| Efficiency | Achieving an outcome with minimum waste of time, cost, effort, and resources | “Did we use resources well?” |

An organization can be effective but inefficient. For example, it may complete a project successfully but exceed the budget and deadline.

> **Final answer:** Effectiveness means achieving the required organizational goal or doing the right work. Efficiency means completing work with minimum waste of time, money, effort, and other resources. Therefore, effectiveness focuses on results, while efficiency focuses on how economically those results are achieved.

## 3. Name and briefly explain the four functions of management.

**How to approach:** State the functions in the correct order and give one concise explanation for each.

**Concept explanation:**

| Function | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Planning | Setting goals and deciding the actions, strategies, and resources required to achieve them |
| Organizing | Arranging tasks, people, authority, responsibilities, and resources |
| Leading | Guiding, communicating with, influencing, and motivating employees |
| Controlling | Monitoring actual performance, comparing it with plans, and taking corrective action |

```text
Planning → Organizing → Leading → Controlling
    ↑                                  ↓
    └────── feedback and adjustment ───┘
```

These functions are ongoing and interrelated. They do not always occur in a strict one-time sequence.

> **Final answer:** The four functions of management are planning, organizing, leading, and controlling. Planning sets goals and determines actions. Organizing arranges tasks, people, authority, and resources. Leading guides and motivates employees toward organizational goals. Controlling monitors results, compares them with plans, and corrects deviations.

## 4. What is Organizational Behavior (OB) and why is it important?

**How to approach:** Define OB, mention its levels of analysis, and explain how it helps managers.

**Concept explanation:** Organizational Behavior studies how individuals, groups, and organizational systems influence behavior and performance in the workplace.

```text
Organizational Behavior
├── Individual level
├── Group / team level
└── Organizational level
```

OB is important because it helps managers understand motivation, communication, leadership, teamwork, conflict, job satisfaction, employee performance, and organizational culture.

**Definition:** Organizational Behavior is the study of how people behave individually and collectively within organizations and how organizational systems affect that behavior.

**Simple meaning:** OB explains why people act as they do at work and how managers can improve workplace performance.

> **Final answer:** Organizational Behavior is the study of how individuals, groups, and organizational structures influence behavior in the workplace. It is important because it helps managers understand and improve motivation, leadership, teamwork, communication, job satisfaction, employee performance, and organizational effectiveness.

## 5. Compare Classical and Behavioral approaches.

**How to approach:** Compare their main focus, view of employees, methods, and strengths rather than describing them separately.

**Concept explanation:**

| Area | Classical approach | Behavioral approach |
|---|---|---|
| Main focus | Structure, rules, specialization, control, and efficiency | People, motivation, relationships, emotions, and group behavior |
| View of employees | Workers are parts of a formal production system | Workers have social and psychological needs |
| Managerial emphasis | Standardization, hierarchy, authority, and productivity | Leadership, communication, participation, and satisfaction |
| Strength | Creates order, consistency, and efficiency | Improves commitment, morale, and human performance |
| Limitation | May ignore human needs and emotions | May give less attention to formal structure and technical efficiency |

**Common Mistake:** The Behavioral approach does not mean that structure and productivity are unimportant. It argues that human factors strongly influence productivity.

> **Final answer:** The Classical approach emphasizes formal structure, division of work, authority, rules, control, and efficiency. It treats the organization mainly as a rational system. The Behavioral approach emphasizes employees’ social and psychological needs, motivation, leadership, communication, and group behavior. Classical theory focuses mainly on tasks and structure, whereas Behavioral theory focuses mainly on people.

## 6. Explain the Contingency approach.

**How to approach:** State the central idea that no single management method is suitable for every situation, then give the factors managers must consider.

**Concept explanation:** The Contingency approach argues that the best management decision depends on the specific situation. Managers should adapt their structure, leadership style, controls, and methods according to factors such as employees, technology, organizational size, task complexity, and the external environment.

```text
Situation is analyzed
        ↓
Relevant factors are identified
        ↓
Management method is selected
        ↓
Results are monitored
        ↓
Method is adjusted when conditions change
```

**Definition:** The Contingency approach is the view that the most suitable management practice depends on the conditions of the particular situation.

**Simple meaning:** There is no single best way to manage every organization or problem.

> **Final answer:** The Contingency approach states that there is no universal management method that works in every situation. Managers must analyze factors such as the environment, employees, technology, task, and organizational size and then select the structure or management style that best fits those conditions.

## 7. What is organizational structure?

**How to approach:** Define structure and identify what it determines inside an organization.

**Concept explanation:** Organizational structure establishes how work is divided, grouped, coordinated, and supervised. It defines roles, responsibilities, authority, reporting relationships, communication channels, and decision-making arrangements.

**Definition:** Organizational structure is the formal arrangement of jobs, responsibilities, authority, communication, and reporting relationships within an organization.

**Simple meaning:** It shows who does what, who reports to whom, and how work is coordinated.

```text
Organizational Structure
├── Jobs and departments
├── Authority and responsibility
├── Reporting relationships
├── Communication channels
└── Decision-making levels
```

> **Final answer:** Organizational structure is the formal system that defines how jobs, responsibilities, authority, departments, communication, and reporting relationships are arranged so that organizational work can be coordinated and goals can be achieved.

## 8. Explain one type of organizational structure with an example.

**How to approach:** Name one structure, explain how employees are grouped, state one benefit, and provide a realistic example.

**Concept explanation:** A **functional structure** groups employees according to specialized business functions such as Finance, Human Resources, Marketing, Operations, and Information Technology.

```text
                 Chief Executive Officer
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
        │                 │                 │
     Finance        Human Resources     Information IT
        │                 │                 │
   Accountants       HR Officers      Developers / Support
```

**Advantages:**

- Develops specialist knowledge.
- Creates clear responsibilities.
- Improves efficiency within each function.

**Possible limitation:** Departments may focus on their own goals and communicate poorly with other functions.

**Example:** A software company may have separate Software Engineering, Quality Assurance, Human Resources, Finance, and Marketing departments.

> **Final answer:** A functional organizational structure groups employees according to specialist functions such as Finance, HR, Marketing, and IT. For example, a software company may have separate Engineering, Quality Assurance, Finance, HR, and Marketing departments. This structure develops specialization and clear responsibility, although communication between departments may become difficult.

## 9. What is power in organizations? Give one example.

**How to approach:** Define power as the ability to influence and give a specific type of power with a workplace example.

**Concept explanation:** Power is the capacity to influence decisions, behavior, work, resources, or outcomes. It may come from formal position, rewards, punishment, expertise, personal charisma, information, or connections.

| Power source | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legitimate / position power | Influence from a formal organizational role |
| Reward power | Ability to provide valued rewards |
| Coercive power | Ability to punish or apply negative consequences |
| Expert power | Influence based on knowledge or skill |
| Referent power | Influence based on respect or admiration |
| Information power | Control of useful information |

**Example:** A senior cybersecurity engineer may influence security decisions because of deep technical knowledge. This is expert power.

> **Final answer:** Power in organizations is the ability of a person or group to influence decisions, behavior, resources, or outcomes. For example, a senior software architect may use expert power to influence the technical design of a project because other employees trust the architect’s knowledge and experience.

## 10. What is motivation?

**How to approach:** Define motivation and explain its three main dimensions: direction, effort, and persistence.

**Concept explanation:** Motivation includes the internal drives and external influences that initiate, direct, and sustain behavior.

```text
Motivation
├── Direction   → What goal or activity a person chooses
├── Effort      → How much energy the person applies
└── Persistence → How long the person continues
```

**Definition:** Motivation is the set of forces that initiates, directs, and sustains a person’s effort toward achieving a goal.

**Simple meaning:** Motivation explains why a person starts working, how hard the person works, and how long the effort continues.

> **Final answer:** Motivation is the internal and external force that initiates, directs, and sustains a person’s behavior toward achieving a goal. It determines the direction of effort, the amount of effort, and the persistence of that effort.

## 11. What is the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation?

**How to approach:** State the source of each type of motivation and give one example of each.

**Concept explanation:**

| Type | Source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic motivation | Enjoyment, interest, learning, personal satisfaction, or meaning from the task itself | A developer learns a new framework because solving the problem is enjoyable |
| Extrinsic motivation | External rewards, recognition, grades, promotion, salary, or avoidance of punishment | An employee completes a task to receive a bonus |

**Common Mistake:** Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation can operate together. An employee may enjoy a task and also value salary or recognition.

> **Final answer:** Intrinsic motivation comes from internal satisfaction, enjoyment, interest, or personal growth gained from performing the activity itself. Extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards or pressures such as salary, bonuses, grades, recognition, promotion, or punishment. For example, learning programming for enjoyment is intrinsic, while learning it to obtain a promotion is extrinsic.

## 12. Briefly explain Maslow’s and Herzberg’s theories.

**How to approach:** Explain Maslow as a hierarchy of needs and Herzberg as two groups of workplace factors. End by distinguishing the main purpose of each theory.

**Concept explanation:**

### Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs

Maslow proposes that people are motivated by five levels of needs:

```text
                 Self-actualization
              Personal growth, potential
                         ▲
                      Esteem
             Recognition, status, achievement
                         ▲
                      Social
             Friendship, belonging, relationships
                         ▲
                      Safety
            Security, stability, protection
                         ▲
                   Physiological
               Food, rest, basic survival
```

Lower-level needs generally require sufficient satisfaction before higher-level needs become stronger motivators.

### Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory

Herzberg divides workplace factors into two categories:

| Hygiene factors | Motivators |
|---|---|
| Salary | Achievement |
| Company policy | Recognition |
| Supervision | Responsibility |
| Working conditions | Advancement |
| Job security | Growth |
| Interpersonal relations | Meaningful work |

- **Hygiene factors** prevent dissatisfaction but do not necessarily create strong motivation.
- **Motivators** create satisfaction, commitment, and stronger performance.

### Main Difference

| Maslow | Herzberg |
|---|---|
| Explains motivation using a hierarchy of human needs | Explains workplace satisfaction using hygiene factors and motivators |
| Includes needs inside and outside work | Focuses mainly on the work environment and job content |
| Suggests movement from lower to higher needs | Separates causes of dissatisfaction from causes of satisfaction |

> **Final answer:** Maslow’s theory states that people are motivated through a hierarchy of physiological, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualization needs. Lower-level needs generally become important before higher-level needs. Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory separates hygiene factors, such as salary, policies, supervision, and working conditions, from motivators, such as achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth, and meaningful work. Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction, while motivators create satisfaction and stronger motivation.

# Major Topic Exam Sections

## Management and the Four Functions

**Exam Tip:** Use the keywords **planning, organizing, leading, controlling, people, resources, and organizational goals**.

**Common Mistake:** Do not present the four functions as completely separate steps. They are ongoing and interrelated.

**Likely Question 1:** Define management according to Henri Fayol.  
**Expected answer:** Management is planning, organizing, leading, and controlling members and resources to achieve organizational goals.

**Likely Question 2:** Distinguish planning from controlling.  
**Expected answer:** Planning sets goals and expected actions; controlling compares actual performance against those plans and corrects deviations.

## Efficiency, Effectiveness, and Organizational Behavior

**Exam Tip:** Efficiency concerns resource use; effectiveness concerns goal achievement. OB must be explained at individual, team, and organizational levels.

**Common Mistake:** “Efficient” does not automatically mean “effective.” Work may be performed cheaply but fail to achieve the required goal.

**Likely Question 1:** Can an organization be effective but inefficient? Explain.  
**Expected answer:** Yes. It may achieve the goal but use excessive time, money, or resources.

**Likely Question 2:** Why should managers study OB?  
**Expected answer:** To improve motivation, communication, teamwork, leadership, job satisfaction, and performance.

## Management Approaches

**Exam Tip:** Compare Classical and Behavioral approaches by focus, employee view, and management techniques. State that Contingency rejects one universal method.

**Common Mistake:** Do not describe the Contingency approach as random decision-making. It requires systematic analysis of situational factors.

**Likely Question 1:** Compare Classical and Behavioral management.  
**Expected answer:** Classical focuses on structure and efficiency; Behavioral focuses on people and motivation.

**Likely Question 2:** What is the central idea of Contingency theory?  
**Expected answer:** The best management method depends on the specific situation.

## Organizational Structure and Power

**Exam Tip:** Structure answers should mention roles, authority, reporting, coordination, and communication. Power answers should name the source of power.

**Common Mistake:** Authority and power are related but not identical. Authority is a formal right attached to a role; power is the broader ability to influence.

**Likely Question 1:** Define organizational structure.  
**Expected answer:** The formal arrangement of tasks, roles, authority, communication, and reporting relationships.

**Likely Question 2:** Give an example of expert power.  
**Expected answer:** A senior engineer influences a technical decision because of specialized knowledge.

## Motivation, Maslow, and Herzberg

**Exam Tip:** Motivation includes direction, effort, and persistence. For Herzberg, write: **hygiene prevents dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction**.

**Common Mistake:** Salary is mainly a hygiene factor in Herzberg’s model; it does not automatically create long-term motivation.

**Likely Question 1:** Differentiate intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.  
**Expected answer:** Intrinsic comes from the task itself; extrinsic comes from external rewards or pressures.

**Likely Question 2:** Compare Maslow and Herzberg.  
**Expected answer:** Maslow organizes human needs into a hierarchy, whereas Herzberg separates workplace hygiene factors from motivators.

# Full Tutorial Summary

Management is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling people and resources to achieve organizational goals. Effective managers achieve the correct objectives, while efficient managers use time, money, effort, and other resources with minimum waste.

The four functions of management form a continuous cycle. Planning establishes goals and actions. Organizing arranges tasks, resources, authority, and relationships. Leading guides and motivates people. Controlling monitors results and takes corrective action.

Organizational Behavior studies how individuals, groups, and organizational systems affect workplace behavior and performance. It helps managers understand motivation, communication, leadership, teamwork, conflict, satisfaction, and organizational effectiveness.

The Classical management approach emphasizes formal structure, specialization, rules, hierarchy, control, and efficiency. The Behavioral approach emphasizes people, relationships, motivation, communication, and group behavior. The Contingency approach states that no single method is best for every situation; practices should fit the environment, task, people, technology, and organizational conditions.

Organizational structure defines how jobs, departments, responsibility, authority, communication, and reporting relationships are arranged. A functional structure groups employees by specialized business functions. Power is the ability to influence behavior, decisions, resources, or outcomes and may arise from position, rewards, punishment, expertise, respect, information, or connections.

Motivation is the force that initiates, directs, and sustains effort. Intrinsic motivation comes from internal satisfaction, while extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards or pressure. Maslow explains motivation using five levels of needs. Herzberg separates hygiene factors that prevent dissatisfaction from motivators that create satisfaction.

# Likely Exam Questions with Short Expected Answers

## Question 1

**Question:** Why must managers balance effectiveness and efficiency?

**Expected answer:** Effectiveness ensures the correct goals are achieved, while efficiency ensures resources are not wasted. Strong management requires both.

## Question 2

**Question:** Explain why management functions are interrelated.

**Expected answer:** Decisions and results from one function affect the others. Monitoring may change plans, organizing affects leadership, and feedback continuously updates the process.

## Question 3

**Question:** Why is the Behavioral approach important?

**Expected answer:** It recognizes that motivation, relationships, emotions, leadership, and group behavior strongly affect employee performance.

## Question 4

**Question:** Give a situation where the Contingency approach is useful.

**Expected answer:** A manager may use a directive style during a system outage but a participative style during normal product planning because the situations require different responses.

## Question 5

**Question:** Differentiate authority and power.

**Expected answer:** Authority is the formal right to make decisions attached to a position. Power is the broader capacity to influence people, decisions, or outcomes and may exist without formal authority.

## Question 6

**Question:** Why do hygiene factors not guarantee motivation?

**Expected answer:** They reduce dissatisfaction, but strong satisfaction and motivation normally arise from achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth, and meaningful work.
